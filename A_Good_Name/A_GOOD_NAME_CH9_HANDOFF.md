## What Just Happened in *Strength and Dignity*

A new chapter was inserted into *Strength and Dignity* as **Chapter 9: "What to Expect from a Young Man Who Fears God."** It pairs with Chapter 8 ("He Is Somebody's Son") to form a *treat / be-treated* teaching unit within Part Three.

The reasoning: Chapter 8 already taught young women how to treat the young men around them. What was missing was the corresponding teaching on what those young women should *expect* from a young man — and what they should walk away from. Without that, the book was lopsided.

The same gap exists in *A Good Name.* Chapter 8 there ("She Is Somebody's Daughter") teaches young men how to treat young women. What's missing is the corresponding teaching on what a young man should expect from a young woman — and what he should walk away from.

## How the Tamar Question Was Handled in the Mirror Chapter (Apply Equivalently)

In *Strength and Dignity,* the Tamar passage (2 Samuel 13) was handled at the "brief" level — the assault was named in one phrase ("she was being violated by her own half-brother — a man who should have protected her"), and the weight of the section sat on Tamar's words and the vocabulary God preserved for young women: *disgraceful thing*, *fool in Israel.* No graphic detail. Age-appropriate but unflinching about the moral category.

For *A Good Name,* apply the same restraint to Proverbs 7. The chapter pictures a young man taken in by a woman who has already decided to seduce him. Don't dwell on the sensual imagery; focus on the warning Scripture itself supplies — the young man pictured as one of "the naive," the path described as "the way to Sheol, descending to the chambers of death" (Proverbs 7:27). Same teaching purpose: Scripture itself gives young men a category and a vocabulary for what they should refuse.

## Things to Avoid (Hard Constraints)

These came up during the *Strength and Dignity* work and apply book-wide:

- **Psalm 119:105** — use no more than once per book, and only when there is good reason. Check existing chapters before deciding whether to use it in the new chapter at all.
- **"Should stop you in your tracks"** — not in Paul's vocabulary. Don't use it of himself, of the reader, or of anyone else. If a similar rhetorical move is needed, write it differently ("Read it slowly" / "It deserves slow reading" / "Sit with it").
- **"Love life"** — not appropriate for the teen audience. Don't use it.
- **Speculative or potentially inappropriate language about biblical characters** — keep all character treatment grounded in what the text actually says.
- **Denominational language for NT roles** — never use "pastor" as a synonym for preacher or evangelist. The *poimen* (pastor/shepherd) is the work of elders; preachers and evangelists are distinct roles. *Diakonon* applied to a woman (e.g., Phoebe) describes service, not the office.
- **Tithing language** — NT giving is proportional and purposeful (1 Cor 16:1–2; 2 Cor 9:7), not tithing.
- **Headship language** — different roles assigned to qualified men (elder, deacon, preacher) reflect God's design for headship, not superior worth. Galatians 3:28 — equal in worth, equal in salvation. This will likely come up only obliquely in this chapter, but the framing must be right if it does.
