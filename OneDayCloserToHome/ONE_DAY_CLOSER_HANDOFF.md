## CORRECTIONS AND DECISIONS MADE

### Chapters 1–4 (from previous sessions)

#### Chapter 2 — Age of Jesus at the Temple Presentation
- **Original text:** "holding a six-week-old baby"
- **Corrected to:** "holding a forty-day-old baby — presented according to the purification requirements of Leviticus 12"
- **Reason:** The presentation at the temple (Luke 2:22-38) occurred after the purification period prescribed in Leviticus 12:2-4 (7 days unclean + 33 days in blood of purification = 40 days), NOT at the circumcision on the eighth day (Luke 2:21). These are two separate events.

#### Chapter 3 — Removal of "Evangelist" Applied to Anna
- **Original text:** "She became, in that moment, a proclaimer. An evangelist."
- **Corrected to:** "She became, in that moment, a proclaimer."
- **Reason:** "Evangelist" (euangelistēs) is a specific NT term used for men — Philip (Acts 21:8), Timothy's work (2 Tim 4:5). The text does not apply the term to Anna.

#### Chapter 3 — Voice Slip: "Your Reader" → Direct Address
- **Corrected to** direct "you" address. The book speaks TO the reader, not ABOUT the reader.

#### Chapter 1 — Paul's Personal Addition
- Paul added: "My father-in-law used to say that growing old wasn't for the faint of heart, and he was right."

#### Chapter 3 — Anna's Age Ambiguity
- The Greek allows two readings: Anna was 84 years old, OR she had been a widow for 84 years. Both possibilities named honestly. Chapter works with the NASB rendering.

### Chapters 5–9 (this session)

#### Chapter 5 — Passage Boundary with Chapter 10
- Chapter 5 quotes 2 Corinthians 4:16-18 in full and works the *paradox* (decay + renewal). It explicitly defers the *scale* work (momentary affliction vs. eternal glory) to Chapter 10: "What Paul does with the *scale* of suffering and glory is a different work from what he does here, and we'll take the time it deserves."
- Chapter 10 must honor this boundary — don't repeat the paradox exposition, focus on the math of suffering vs. glory.

#### Chapter 5 — Transition Marker
- Chapter 5 explicitly names the shift from OT narrative examples (Simeon, Anna, Caleb) to Paul's direct NT teaching. This is the hinge of the book's structure.

#### Chapter 6 — Honest Handling of the Intermediate State
- The chapter states what the text affirms: the believer who dies is conscious, with Christ, in a state described as "better" and "at home."
- The chapter draws a firm line: the specific nature of the intermediate state (what it feels like, how time is experienced, what we are aware of) is not detailed by the text. "Where the text is silent, we should be too."
- No speculation about what heaven "looks like." No near-death-experience theology. No importing Revelation imagery into a Pauline context.

#### Chapter 6 — *Katapino* Thread Established
- 2 Cor 5:4: "what is mortal will be swallowed up by life" — *katapino*
- This word recurs in Chapter 7 (1 Cor 15:54: "Death is swallowed up in victory")
- The thread is explicitly connected in Chapter 7's exposition.

#### Chapter 7 — *Pneumatikos* Clarification
- "Spiritual body" (*pneumatikos*) does NOT mean immaterial or ghostlike. It means animated by the Spirit, empowered by God's Spirit, suited to eternal existence. The resurrection body is *more* physical, not less.

#### Chapter 7 — Seed Metaphor as Structural Image
- The burial = planting reframe is the chapter's central image. The closing line lands it: "The body that aches while you do it is a seed. And what's coming out of the ground is glory."

#### Chapter 8 — Tent Imagery Thread
- Abraham "dwelling in tents" (Heb 11:9) is explicitly connected to Paul's tent metaphor from Chapter 6 (2 Cor 5:1-4). The thread is named in the text.

#### Chapter 8 — Rearview Mirror Callback
- The chapter explicitly connects Hebrews 11:15 ("if they had been thinking of that country from which they went out, they would have had opportunity to return") to the rearview mirror theme from Chapter 1. The text says: "The road goes both ways."

#### Chapter 9 — Slavery vs. Apprehension Distinction
- The chapter carefully distinguishes between being freed from the *slavery* of the fear of death and never feeling any apprehension about dying. References Jesus in Gethsemane (Matt 26:38) and Paul's groaning (2 Cor 5) to establish that the weight of mortality is real even for the faithful.
- The soldier analogy: "There is a difference between a soldier who feels fear before a battle and a man who is so paralyzed by fear that he cannot leave his house."

#### Chapter 9 — Thread Convergence
- Chapter 9 explicitly summarizes Chapters 5-8 in a single paragraph, showing how each truth is "a wall between you and the fear of death." This is the first time the book steps back and shows the reader the architecture of the argument being built across chapters.

## GUARDRAILS ESTABLISHED DURING WRITING

### 1. Do Not Apply Male-Specific NT Roles/Terms to Women
Established after the "evangelist" correction in Chapter 3. When the NT assigns specific terms to men — elder, deacon, evangelist, preacher — those are not to be applied to women. Describe what women in the text DID without assigning titles the text doesn't give them. This is a firm rule for the entire book.

**Paul's note:** "Let's try very hard not to use biblical terms that are reserved in the New Testament for men and apply them to women. I think that is a big reason the denominations have drifted so far — they have gotten careless and it just keeps getting more careless as time goes by."

### 2. All Theological Guardrails from the Project File Remain Active
- Scripture interprets Scripture (immediate context → broader context → cross-references)
- Old Testament / New Testament distinction maintained throughout
- No thief-on-the-cross template (Heb. 9:16-17)
- NASB preferred; Greek/Hebrew discussed when it illuminates meaning
- Honest handling — if a passage is difficult, say so
- CENI framework respected
- Denominational neutrality — no denominational jargon, titles, or concepts

### 3. Voice Consistency
The book speaks directly to the reader ("you"). Avoid slipping into author-to-assistant voice ("your reader," "the reader should feel..."). The reader is always "you."

### 4. Word Count
No target word count per chapter. Let each chapter say what it needs to say and stop. Don't stretch to hit a number, don't cut short to stay under one. Paul's instruction: "I don't want it to feel contrived — I want it to flow naturally."

### 5. Honest Handling of Textual Questions
When a passage has genuine ambiguity, name both possibilities honestly, explain why we're taking the reading we take, and don't pretend the question doesn't exist.

### 6. Greek/Hebrew Woven Naturally
Word studies and original language observations are part of the text's flow — not displayed academically, not footnoted, but illuminating meaning for the everyday reader.

### 7. Where the Text Is Silent, We Are Silent
Established explicitly in Chapter 6's handling of the intermediate state. When Scripture does not detail something, we do not speculate. We state what we know, draw the line, and stop.

## TONE AND STYLE (CONFIRMED IN PRACTICE)

- **Warm but honest.** Real losses honored before hope is spoken into them.
- **Conversational.** Written for someone in a chair with coffee, not a seminary classroom.
- **Narrative-driven.** Each chapter tells a story from Scripture — the reader walks into the scene.
- **Sensory and physical.** Simeon's weathered hands, Caleb charging the hill, the tent fabric wearing thin, the smell of dirt at a graveside. Put the reader *there*.
- **Accessible but precise.** Greek and Hebrew are tools for clarity, not displays of learning.
- **No denominational jargon.** Scripture speaks for itself.
- **Each chapter connects back to the central reframe:** Stop looking back, start looking forward. Every day is one day closer to home.
- **Chapter closings echo the chapter theme** in a short, direct, personal way and end with "One day closer to home."
