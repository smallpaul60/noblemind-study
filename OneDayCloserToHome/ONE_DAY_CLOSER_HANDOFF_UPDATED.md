## CORRECTIONS AND DECISIONS MADE

### Chapters 1–4 (from earlier sessions)

#### Chapter 2 — Age of Jesus at the Temple Presentation
- **Original text:** "holding a six-week-old baby"
- **Corrected to:** "holding a forty-day-old baby — presented according to the purification requirements of Leviticus 12"
- **Reason:** The presentation at the temple (Luke 2:22-38) occurred after the purification period prescribed in Leviticus 12:2-4 (7 days unclean + 33 days in blood of purification = 40 days), NOT at the circumcision on the eighth day (Luke 2:21). These are two separate events.

#### Chapter 3 — Removal of "Evangelist" Applied to Anna
- **Original text:** "She became, in that moment, a proclaimer. An evangelist."
- **Corrected to:** "She became, in that moment, a proclaimer."
- **Reason:** "Evangelist" (euangelistēs) is a specific NT term used for men — Philip (Acts 21:8), Timothy's work (2 Tim 4:5). The text does not apply the term to Anna.

#### Chapter 3 — Voice Slip: "Your Reader" → Direct Address
- **Corrected to** direct "you" address. The book speaks TO the reader, not ABOUT the reader.

#### Chapter 1 — Paul's Personal Addition
- Paul added: "My father-in-law used to say that growing old wasn't for the faint of heart, and he was right."

#### Chapter 3 — Anna's Age Ambiguity
- The Greek allows two readings: Anna was 84 years old, OR she had been a widow for 84 years. Both possibilities named honestly. Chapter works with the NASB rendering.

### Chapters 5–9 (from earlier sessions)

#### Chapter 5 — Passage Boundary with Chapter 10
- Chapter 5 quotes 2 Corinthians 4:16-18 in full and works the *paradox* (decay + renewal). It explicitly defers the *scale* work (momentary affliction vs. eternal glory) to Chapter 10: "What Paul does with the *scale* of suffering and glory is a different work from what he does here, and we'll take the time it deserves."
- Chapter 10 honors this boundary — doesn't repeat the paradox exposition, focuses on the math of suffering vs. glory.

#### Chapter 5 — Transition Marker
- Chapter 5 explicitly names the shift from OT narrative examples (Simeon, Anna, Caleb) to Paul's direct NT teaching. This is the hinge of the book's structure.

#### Chapter 6 — Honest Handling of the Intermediate State
- The chapter states what the text affirms: the believer who dies is conscious, with Christ, in a state described as "better" and "at home."
- The chapter draws a firm line: the specific nature of the intermediate state (what it feels like, how time is experienced, what we are aware of) is not detailed by the text. "Where the text is silent, we should be too."
- No speculation about what heaven "looks like." No near-death-experience theology. No importing Revelation imagery into a Pauline context.

#### Chapter 6 — *Katapino* Thread Established
- 2 Cor 5:4: "what is mortal will be swallowed up by life" — *katapino*
- This word recurs in Chapter 7 (1 Cor 15:54: "Death is swallowed up in victory")
- The thread is explicitly connected in Chapter 7's exposition.

#### Chapter 7 — *Pneumatikos* Clarification
- "Spiritual body" (*pneumatikos*) does NOT mean immaterial or ghostlike. It means animated by the Spirit, empowered by God's Spirit, suited to eternal existence. The resurrection body is *more* physical, not less.

#### Chapter 7 — Seed Metaphor as Structural Image
- The burial = planting reframe is the chapter's central image. The closing line lands it: "The body that aches while you do it is a seed. And what's coming out of the ground is glory."

#### Chapter 8 — Tent Imagery Thread
- Abraham "dwelling in tents" (Heb 11:9) is explicitly connected to Paul's tent metaphor from Chapter 6 (2 Cor 5:1-4). The thread is named in the text.

#### Chapter 8 — Rearview Mirror Callback
- The chapter explicitly connects Hebrews 11:15 ("if they had been thinking of that country from which they went out, they would have had opportunity to return") to the rearview mirror theme from Chapter 1. The text says: "The road goes both ways."

#### Chapter 9 — Slavery vs. Apprehension Distinction
- The chapter carefully distinguishes between being freed from the *slavery* of the fear of death and never feeling any apprehension about dying. References Jesus in Gethsemane (Matt 26:38) and Paul's groaning (2 Cor 5) to establish that the weight of mortality is real even for the faithful.
- The soldier analogy: "There is a difference between a soldier who feels fear before a battle and a man who is so paralyzed by fear that he cannot leave his house."

#### Chapter 9 — Thread Convergence
- Chapter 9 explicitly summarizes Chapters 5-8 in a single paragraph, showing how each truth is "a wall between you and the fear of death." This is the first time the book steps back and shows the reader the architecture of the argument being built across chapters.

### Chapters 10–13 (final session — March 13, 2026)

#### Chapter 10 — Passage Boundary with Chapter 5 Honored
- Chapter 10 acknowledges the earlier treatment of 2 Cor 4:16 ("In Chapter 5, we looked at Paul's paradox...") and explicitly pivots: "But Paul wasn't finished. He went on to do something different... It's a comparison. A weighing."
- Chapter 10 does NOT re-quote verse 16 or re-work the decay/renewal theme. It picks up at verse 17 and stays there. The chapter is about the *scale* — suffering weighed against glory — not the paradox.

#### Chapter 10 — Paul's Suffering Catalog as Evidence
- 2 Corinthians 11:23-28 is used to establish what Paul was calling "light" — five lashings, three beatings, stoning, shipwrecks, constant danger, sleeplessness, hunger. The reader sees the weight of what Paul endured and then hears him call it light. The credibility of the claim rests on the evidence of his life.

#### Chapter 10 — *Katergazetai* (Producing)
- The chapter highlights that affliction is not merely endured before glory arrives — it is *producing* the glory (*katergazetai*). Connected to James 1:3 and 1 Peter 1:7 to show the consistent NT testimony that trials borne in faith are productive, not wasted.

#### Chapter 10 — *Proskaira* Thread Established
- The word *proskaira* (temporary, lasting for a season) is introduced in Chapter 10's exposition of verse 18. This term is picked up in Chapter 11 when "the first things have passed away" is connected to Paul's temporal/eternal distinction.

#### Chapter 11 — Apocalyptic Genre Handled Carefully
- The chapter describes what Revelation 21:1-7 says without over-interpreting symbolic elements. No speculation about "no longer any sea." No importing imagery from other parts of Revelation. The declarative statements (God with men, tears wiped, death gone, all things new) are treated as plain and let to speak plainly.

#### Chapter 11 — *Skēnē* / Tent Thread Transformed
- The *skēnē* (tabernacle) of God in Rev 21:3 is explicitly connected to the *skēnos* (tent) of 2 Cor 5 from Chapter 6. Same root, opposite direction: the frail human tent gives way; God's permanent tabernacle arrives. Also connected to *skēnoō* in John 1:14 — the incarnation was temporary tabernacling; Rev 21 is permanent.

#### Chapter 11 — *Kainos* vs. *Neos* Distinction
- "New" in Rev 21:1 and 21:5 is *kainos* (new in quality/character), not *neos* (new in time/recently made). God is not scrapping creation — He is renewing it. Connected to the resurrection body pattern from Chapter 7: continuity and transformation, not annihilation and replacement.

#### Chapter 11 — *Exaleiphō* and Psalm 56:8
- "Wipe away" (*exaleiphō*) is physical — God close enough to touch the reader's face. Connected to Psalm 56:8 (tears in God's bottle, counted in His book) to establish that the tears were real, seen, and recorded.

#### Chapter 11 — *Katapino* Thread Completed
- "Death is swallowed up in victory" (1 Cor 15:54) referenced in the Rev 21:4 exposition — the *katapino* thread from Chapters 6 and 7 reaches its final fulfillment: "Death is no more."

#### Chapter 11 — "First Things" as Gathering Category
- Everything the reader is enduring is classified as "first things" (Rev 21:4). Connected to *proskaira* from Chapter 10. "They feel permanent when you're in the middle of them. But they are first things. And first things have an expiration date."

#### Chapter 12 — Gospel Presentation: Belief → Mark 16:16 → Repentance → Confession → Baptism
- The chapter walks through the NT response to the gospel in order:
  1. **Belief** — John 8:24. Foundation, but not the whole building. James 2:19 (demons believe).
  2. **Jesus' own command** — Mark 16:16. "He who has believed and has been baptized shall be saved." Believed AND baptized — joined by Jesus, inseparable.
  3. **Repentance** — Acts 2:37-38. *Metanoeō*. A change of mind producing a change of direction. Acts 17:30.
  4. **Baptism** — Romans 6:3-5. Burial and resurrection with Christ. The transition point. Not symbolic. Not optional.
  5. **Every blessing in Christ** — Ephesians 1:3 (every spiritual blessing is *in Christ*). Galatians 3:27 (baptized *into* Christ). The only door the NT describes.
  6. **Confession** — Romans 10:9-10. Belief becomes public. Ethiopian's confession in Acts 8:36-39.
  7. **The title verse** — Acts 22:16. Saul believed for three days, prayed for three days, and his sins were still there. "Wash away your sins" happened in baptism.
  8. **The pattern** — Acts 2 (Pentecost, that day), Acts 8 (Ethiopian, desert road), Acts 16 (jailer, that very hour), Acts 22 (Saul, no more delay). Every conversion immediate. Every one includes baptism.

#### Chapter 12 — Mark 16:16 Addition (Paul's Request)
- Added after the belief section. Jesus Himself joined belief and baptism in one sentence: "He who has believed and has been baptized shall be saved." The chapter states: "What Jesus has joined, no man has the authority to separate."

#### Chapter 12 — Ephesians 1:3 / Galatians 3:27 Addition (Paul's Request)
- Added after the Romans 6 exposition. Every spiritual blessing is *in Christ* (Eph 1:3). Baptism is how you get *into* Christ (Gal 3:27). The book's promises (building, imperishable body, city, tears wiped away) are all named and located *in Him*.

#### Chapter 12 — No Thief on the Cross
- Not mentioned, not needed. The positive evidence from Acts is so consistent and so clear that the objection doesn't need to be raised.

#### Chapter 13 — The Circle Closes
- Opens where Chapter 1 opened: Paul in a Roman prison, Philippians 3:13-14. "When you read those words in Chapter 1, they were an introduction... Now you know what he was straining toward."
- Every thread gathered in a building wave: Simeon, Anna, Caleb, the paradox, the tent, the seed, Abraham's city, the fear broken, the math, the tears wiped away.
- Brief callback to Chapter 12 for the reader who hasn't yet obeyed.
- Addresses the faithful believer: the race isn't over, the last laps matter, the call is still upward.
- Closes with the same words from Chapter 1: "Turn around." / "The sunrise ahead is better than anything behind you." / "One day closer to home."

## GUARDRAILS ESTABLISHED DURING WRITING

### 1. Do Not Apply Male-Specific NT Roles/Terms to Women
Established after the "evangelist" correction in Chapter 3. When the NT assigns specific terms to men — elder, deacon, evangelist, preacher — those are not to be applied to women. Describe what women in the text DID without assigning titles the text doesn't give them. This is a firm rule for the entire book.

**Paul's note:** "Let's try very hard not to use biblical terms that are reserved in the New Testament for men and apply them to women. I think that is a big reason the denominations have drifted so far — they have gotten careless and it just keeps getting more careless as time goes by."

### 2. All Theological Guardrails from the Project File Remain Active
- Scripture interprets Scripture (immediate context → broader context → cross-references)
- Old Testament / New Testament distinction maintained throughout
- No thief-on-the-cross template (Heb. 9:16-17)
- NASB preferred; Greek/Hebrew discussed when it illuminates meaning
- Honest handling — if a passage is difficult, say so
- CENI framework respected
- Denominational neutrality — no denominational jargon, titles, or concepts

### 3. Voice Consistency
The book speaks directly to the reader ("you"). Avoid slipping into author-to-assistant voice ("your reader," "the reader should feel..."). The reader is always "you."

### 4. Word Count
No target word count per chapter. Let each chapter say what it needs to say and stop. Don't stretch to hit a number, don't cut short to stay under one. Paul's instruction: "I don't want it to feel contrived — I want it to flow naturally."

### 5. Honest Handling of Textual Questions
When a passage has genuine ambiguity, name both possibilities honestly, explain why we're taking the reading we take, and don't pretend the question doesn't exist.

### 6. Greek/Hebrew Woven Naturally
Word studies and original language observations are part of the text's flow — not displayed academically, not footnoted, but illuminating meaning for the everyday reader.

### 7. Where the Text Is Silent, We Are Silent
Established explicitly in Chapter 6's handling of the intermediate state. When Scripture does not detail something, we do not speculate. We state what we know, draw the line, and stop.

## TONE AND STYLE (CONFIRMED ACROSS ALL 13 CHAPTERS)

- **Warm but honest.** Real losses honored before hope is spoken into them.
- **Conversational.** Written for someone in a chair with coffee, not a seminary classroom.
- **Narrative-driven.** Each chapter tells a story from Scripture — the reader walks into the scene.
- **Sensory and physical.** Simeon's weathered hands, Caleb charging the hill, the tent fabric wearing thin, the smell of dirt at a graveside, the jacket still in the closet, the crackers at the grocery store. Put the reader *there*.
- **Accessible but precise.** Greek and Hebrew are tools for clarity, not displays of learning.
- **No denominational jargon.** Scripture speaks for itself.
- **Each chapter connects back to the central reframe:** Stop looking back, start looking forward. Every day is one day closer to home.
- **Chapter closings echo the chapter theme** in a short, direct, personal way and end with "One day closer to home."

## STRUCTURAL NOTES

### Chapters 1-4: The Examples
1. **Chapter 1** — Establishes the problem (rearview mirror) and the reframe (face forward).
2. **Chapter 2** — Simeon: Aging defined by *anticipation*, not memory.
3. **Chapter 3** — Anna: Aging does not end *usefulness*. It deepens it.
4. **Chapter 4** — Caleb: Faith doesn't become *passive* with age. It charges.

Chapters 2-3 are a natural pair (same scene, same day, complementary themes).
Chapter 4 shifts the energy from quiet waiting/serving to active charging.

### Chapters 5-9: The Theology
5. **Chapter 5** — The paradox: outer decay + inner renewal, simultaneously. (2 Cor 4:16-18)
6. **Chapter 6** — What happens when the body gives out: tent → building, absent from body → at home with Lord. (2 Cor 5:1-8)
7. **Chapter 7** — The resurrection body: burial as planting, perishable raised imperishable. (1 Cor 15:35-58)
8. **Chapter 8** — The destination: Abraham's city, strangers seeking a country, God not ashamed. (Heb 11:8-16)
9. **Chapter 9** — Freedom from fear: death's power broken, slavery ended. (Heb 2:14-15) — Thread convergence paragraph summarizes Ch 5-8.

Chapter 5 marks the transition from OT narrative to NT exposition.
Chapters 5-7 are a Pauline sequence (paradox → death → resurrection).
Chapter 8 shifts to Hebrews for the journey/destination metaphor.
Chapter 9 converges the threads and addresses the reader's deepest fear.

### Chapters 10-13: The Crescendo
10. **Chapter 10** — The scale: momentary affliction vs. eternal weight of glory. (2 Cor 4:17-18)
11. **Chapter 11** — The vision: no more tears, all things new. (Rev 21:1-7)
12. **Chapter 12** — The invitation: gospel presentation for the non-Christian reader. (Acts 22:16, Mark 16:16, Acts 2:38, Romans 6:3-5, Ephesians 1:3, Galatians 3:27)
13. **Chapter 13** — The send-off: press on, all threads gathered. (Phil 3:13-14) — Circle closes to Chapter 1.

Chapter 10 completes the Pauline exposition begun in Chapter 5.
Chapter 11 is the only chapter built primarily on Revelation — genre handled with care.
Chapter 12 shifts tone: gentle but direct, honest but urgent.
Chapter 13 is the shortest chapter — gathers and releases. Says what it needs to say and stops.
