# Before I Formed You

## Project Overview

**Title:** Before I Formed You  
**Authors:** Paul and Pam Hainline  
**Brand:** NobleMind.Study  
**Purpose:** A short booklet written for young women contemplating abortion, designed to be placed in waiting rooms (OB/GYN offices, pregnancy resource centers, crisis pregnancy centers) — especially across Texas where abortion is outlawed and young pregnant women need to hear God's voice in their moment of crisis.  
**Format:** Short enough to read in one sitting; long enough to feel like a real book, not a pamphlet. Something she puts in her purse and comes back to at two in the morning.  
**Translation:** NASB (primary)  
**Methodology:** Scripture alone. Scripture interprets Scripture. No commentaries, no outside sources, no politics, no guilt. Let God's word speak to her.

---

## Tone

Like Pam. A wise, gentle woman sitting beside her who has no agenda except for her to know she's loved. The Titus 2 older woman — not lecturing, not pressuring, just present and honest. Someone who speaks quietly because she doesn't need to raise her voice. Steady, warm, Scripture-saturated truth spoken softly.

No exclamation points. No urgency. No guilt. Just presence.

**Voice discipline:** Trust the reader. Trust the story. Trust the Scripture. Tell the story, drop the verse, and move. Do not explain what the reader should be feeling — the story creates the feeling. Do not announce what matters — just say the thing and let the weight land. Do not interpret the emotion for her. Give the verses a beat of silence after them. A one-line paragraph. Let them breathe. Match the voice of *From the Beginning* — confident, conversational, and quiet enough that the reader leans in rather than being pushed.

---

## The Arc

**You are seen. Your child is known. God has done this before.**

The booklet starts with *her*, not the baby. If she feels unseen, she can't hear anything about the child yet. Once she knows she is seen by God, the turn moves gently to the life she's carrying. Then the heart of the booklet walks her through the stories of biblical women who faced impossible circumstances — and every child they carried had a purpose none of them could see. The close shifts from the moment to the future: what the child may become.

---

## Connection to Other Works

*Before I Formed You* serves as a companion to *From the Beginning: The Gospel from the Ground Up*. Where *Before I Formed You* speaks to her crisis moment and anchors her in God's love for her and her child, *From the Beginning* presents the full gospel message following the Acts 17 model. One plants, another waters. *Before I Formed You* may save the baby's life. *From the Beginning* may reach her soul. They are not the same job.

## Writing Notes

- The child is referred to as "her" in Chapter 2. If the reader knows she is carrying a boy, she can make that translation easily. This avoids the clinical feel of "it."
- Each chapter follows the same structure: introduce the woman, tell her story from Scripture, connect it to the reader's moment, close with the truth she needs to carry forward.
- The thread running through every chapter: God sees the mother, God knows the child, and God's purposes are bigger than what any of them can see in the moment.
- Chapters should remain short enough that the entire booklet can be read in one sitting in a waiting room.
- Trust the reader. Tell the story, let the Scripture land, move on. Do not explain the emotion — the story creates it.
