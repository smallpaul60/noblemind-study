# For Pam

## On Your Seventy-First Birthday

*May 18, 2026*

---

Pam,

Seventy-one years.

You woke up this morning at seventy-one years old. The number sounds bigger than it has any right to. I look at you and I don't see seventy-one — I see the woman I first met more than twenty years ago, and the woman I married on December 8, 2012, and the woman who has made every day since one I would not have traded for anything. But the calendar says seventy-one, and the calendar isn't wrong. It is, however, only telling half the story.

I want to tell you the rest of it today.

When we met, you were married to someone else, and not long after, his life ended in a way none of us saw coming. You walked through a valley I never had to walk. By that point Buck and Danny were grown and on their own, and Christy was a young woman about to start her own marriage — so you didn't carry the weight of small children needing a mother. But you carried something else: the silence of a life that suddenly looked nothing like the one you had planned. And you carried it the way you carry everything — quietly, without complaint, with a kind of steady grace that I have come to recognize as one of the most beautiful things about you. By the time you and I stood before God and family fourteen years ago this December, you had already proven what you were made of. I just had the good sense to know it when I saw it.

The Lord gave me a Proverbs 31 wife. I have known it from the beginning.

> *"An excellent wife, who can find? For her worth is far above jewels. The heart of her husband trusts in her, and he will have no lack of gain. She does him good and not evil all the days of her life."*
> — Proverbs 31:10–12 (NASB)

That isn't poetry to me, Pam. That is a description of you. Word for word. The trust I have in you is one of the few things in this life I have never questioned, not for a single hour. You have done me good and not evil — every day, without exception, for as long as I have known you.

The passage goes on, and so do you. *"She rises also while it is still night, and gives food to her household."* *"She extends her hand to the poor, and she stretches out her hands to the needy."* *"Strength and dignity are her clothing, and she smiles at the future."* You have worked your whole life, Pam. Long before me, alongside me, sometimes when I wished you would rest and you wouldn't. You have given to your children and your grandchildren and now your great-grandchildren, and somehow there has always been more to give.

Buck and Danny and Christy. Eight grandchildren. One great-grandson holding his place in your heart, and another on the way. Look at what God has built through you. The four youngest grandchildren — the ones I love as my own because they were so small when we married — they have a grandmother who has loved them with everything she has, and they know it. The older ones know it too. The great-grandson knows it the way babies know things, in his bones before he can put it in words.

> *"Her children rise up and bless her; her husband also, and he praises her, saying, 'Many daughters have done nobly, but you excel them all.'"*
> — Proverbs 31:28–29 (NASB)

I have not said it enough. I am saying it now.

•  •  •

There is something else I need to say to you today, and I need to say it carefully, because I know you.

You wonder, sometimes, whether you are enough. Whether you have done enough. Whether the meal was good enough, whether the work was good enough, whether your understanding of Scripture is as deep as it should be. I have watched you carry that quiet weight for years. And I want to tell you the truth about it today, on your seventy-first birthday, because the truth matters more than anything else I could give you.

Pam, you are more than enough.

Let me start with the simple, plain truth that I do not think I say to you nearly often enough.

Your meals are not "good enough." They are excellent. They are some of the best food I have ever eaten in my life, and I have eaten my share of meals in this lifetime. The people who sit at your table do not leave thinking, "Well, that was acceptable." They leave thinking about how to get invited back. You have a gift for it, and you have honored that gift for decades by using it to feed your family, your friends, and anyone who walked through the door. Your cooking is not on trial. It never was.

Your work is not "barely enough." It is extraordinary. You out-work people half your age and you do not even notice. I have watched you keep going long after I would have sat down. I have watched you take on jobs nobody asked you to do, finish them, and then look around for what else needs doing. The standard you hold yourself to in physical labor is higher than the standard most people would dare to set, and you meet it day after day. You are not "keeping up." You are setting the pace, and the rest of us are doing our best to follow.

Your understanding of God's word is not "behind." It is the understanding of a woman who has read it, lived it, and trusted it through the kind of life that tests every word of it. You may not have a degree on the wall. You don't need one. You know the One the Book is about, and you have walked with Him longer than many a learned preacher has been alive. That is the understanding that counts.

Hear me, Pam. By every measure that anyone has the right to apply to you — and by every standard you have ever held over your own head — you exceed it.

And then — and only then, because the order matters — there is the deepest truth of all. Your standing before God has never depended on whether the meal was good enough, or whether the work was hard enough, or whether your understanding was deep enough. It has depended on Jesus. He has held you for as long as you have known Him, and He is holding you now.

> *"For by grace you have been saved through faith; and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God; not as a result of works, so that no one may boast."*
> — Ephesians 2:8–9 (NASB)

The Proverbs 31 chapter does not end with the cooking or the cloth or the hands stretched out to the needy. It ends with this:

> *"Charm is deceitful and beauty is vain, but a woman who fears the Lord, she shall be praised."*
> — Proverbs 31:30 (NASB)

That is the verse with your name on it. You fear the Lord. You always have. And He sees it — every day of it, every quiet act of it, every moment when you wondered if anyone noticed. He noticed every single one.

So let me settle two questions at once today, on your seventy-first birthday. The Lord has covered you, and you don't have to earn what He has already given. And in the very same breath: the meals are wonderful, the work is exceptional, the heart is deep, the understanding is true, and the woman I married is more than enough. By a long mile. More than enough.

•  •  •

Now — about the years ahead.

We have been writing a book, you and I, about what aging looks like for those who belong to Christ. The whole point of the book is that the rearview mirror is not where our eyes are supposed to be. We are not living back there. We never have been, you and I — that's one of the things I love about you. You don't sit in the past. You don't grieve what was. You face forward, and you keep moving.

Paul wrote it like this:

> *"Brethren, I do not regard myself as having laid hold of it yet; but one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind and reaching forward to what lies ahead, I press on toward the goal for the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus."*
> — Philippians 3:13–14 (NASB)

That's us. Seventy-one years old today, and we are still pressing on. There is a building waiting for you that no hands have touched. There is a body coming that will not ache and will not tire and will not forget anything you ever wanted to remember. There is a city with foundations, and the architect and builder is God Himself. And every day — including today, your seventy-first — is one day closer to it.

> *"She smiles at the future."*
> — Proverbs 31:25 (NASB)

Keep smiling, Pam. The future is better than anything behind you.

I love you. I have loved you for the best years of my life, and I will love you for every year God gives us, and beyond — into a country where love does not end and tears do not fall and the people we have loved are gathered together again in the presence of the One who loved us first.

Happy seventy-first birthday, my Proverbs 31 wife.

One day closer to home. Together.

— Paul
