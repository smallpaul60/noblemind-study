# Panel 12 — Love hopes all things

**Concept:** Late afternoon at the church grounds. A small fresh garden bed has been dug at the edge of the lawn. The subject — a South Asian teen boy — is kneeling beside a knee-high sapling he has just planted, both hands gently packing the soil around its base. Standing close beside him is a younger Latino boy (around 9), holding a small green watering can with both hands, watching the planting and waiting for his turn. The sapling is tiny and wrapped at the root ball, held upright by a thin wooden stake. The afternoon is quiet. Golden-hour light slants across them.

**Why this composition:** The Greek *panta elpizei* — "all things it hopes" — sits between *believes* and *endures* in Paul's list. The plan doc's instinct is right: action-based hope (a tree planted today that will not be a tree for years) reads far stronger than mood-based hope (a wistful gaze at a sunrise). Per [[smallest-physical-fix-first]], the verse carrier is **the tiny sapling itself**. No one plants a tree this small unless they believe in a future they trust. The smallness of the tree is the size of the hope.

**Why love-not-just-hope:** The verse is in the love passage. The hope here must be directed *toward another person*, not just generally optimistic. The presence of the younger boy is what does the love-work: the subject isn't planting for himself, he is planting for someone who will play under this tree someday. The two of them — older and younger — together tending the same small future — makes "love hopes" visible rather than just "person hopes."

**Why the younger boy and not another teen:** A peer partner would read as just two friends doing a service project. A *younger* boy watching the older one plant carries the multigenerational weight that *hope* needs — the older teen is teaching the younger one what hope looks like by doing it in front of him. The next generation receives the hope.

**Why hands on the dirt and not standing back admiring:** Hands packing soil around the sapling's base is gentle, intentional, invested. A teen standing back admiring his work has already finished hoping. The verse is in the *act of planting*, not the *finished planting*.

**Distinct from panel 13 (Love endures all things):** Hopes = invests in a future not yet visible. Endures = sustains through a long present. Both are time-oriented but in different directions — hope reaches forward, endurance holds the present.

**Portrait orientation:** Two figures crouched/standing beside a small tree with sky above is naturally portrait. Push it again.

## Note before you generate

Five specific failure modes to watch for:

1. **The tree is too big.** If Gemini renders a mature tree or even a substantial young tree, the verse evaporates — the hope is in the *smallness*. Regen note: `the sapling is TINY — knee-high, slim trunk no thicker than a thumb, only a few small branches and leaves. Held upright by a thin wooden stake. Its smallness is unmistakable.`

2. **The subject is standing back, finished, admiring.** The verse is in the *act of planting* — hands in the soil. Regen note: `the subject is kneeling beside the sapling with both hands packing the soil around its base, hands cupped around the small trunk. Not standing, not stepping back, not admiring — hands actively in the dirt.`

3. **The younger boy is bored, looking away, or absent.** His attentive watching is the love-half of the verse — the next generation receiving the hope. Regen note: `the younger boy is standing close beside the kneeling teen, holding a small watering can with both hands, watching the older boy's hands at work with quiet attentive interest. Not bored, not distracted — engaged in the moment.`

4. **The expressions are too bright / staged-stock-photo.** "Love hopes all things" is a quiet absorbed hope, not a beaming-at-the-camera moment. Regen note: `the subject's face is calm and absorbed in the work — small soft natural expression, not smiling at the camera, not posing. The younger boy is quietly attentive, not grinning.`

5. **Landscape instead of portrait.** Push portrait again. Regen note: `the frame must be PORTRAIT orientation — taller than wide. Vertical camera. Warm sky fills the upper third of the frame above the kneeling figure.`
