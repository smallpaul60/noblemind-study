# Panel 11 — Love believes all things

**Concept:** Late afternoon, two teenage girls sitting close together on a low brick wall or bench outside the school. The friend — a white teen girl — is mid-explanation, body slightly tentative, telling the subject something that could easily be doubted: an apology, a reason she didn't show up earlier, a difficult thing that happened, a hard truth about what she did or didn't do. The subject — a Korean-American teen girl — is listening with her body fully open: uncrossed arms, leaning slightly toward her friend, eyes engaged. Her right hand is reaching across to rest on her friend's hand on the bench between them — a small touch that says *I hear you, and I believe you*.

**Why this composition:** The Greek *panta pisteuei* — "all things it believes" — does not mean "love is gullible." It means love thinks the best, gives the benefit of the doubt, and takes another at her word until there is real reason not to. The verse is about *the moment of choosing to trust* when the moment could have gone the other way. Per [[smallest-physical-fix-first]], the verse carrier is **the subject's open body posture combined with her reaching hand on her friend's hand**. The open body is the belief; the hand is the seal of it.

**Why the friend looks slightly tentative:** Without that small hint of "are you going to believe me?" in the friend's posture, the moment becomes generic listening. The friend's slight anxiety — knowing what she has just said could be doubted — is what makes the subject's believing posture a *gift*. The viewer reads it as "she could have pressed for proof; she chose not to."

**Why a hand on a hand and not a shoulder:** A shoulder touch is comfort (panel 10 territory). A hand-on-hand touch on the bench between two seated friends reads as agreement, as covenant, as *we're good, I believe you*. The geometry of the touch carries a different verse than the geometry of panel 10.

**Distinct from panel 8 (no scorekeeping) and panel 10 (bears all things):**
- Panel 8: post-conflict, working side-by-side on a shared task. The forgiveness is in the geometry of shared workspace.
- Panel 10: friend is under a weight, subject sits beside with a steady hand and outward gaze. The bearing is in the steady presence.
- Panel 11: friend is *telling* the subject something that could be doubted; subject's response *to the telling* is to believe. The verse is in the moment of reception.

**Why same gender:** Two girls on a bench with one's hand on the other's hand reads as covenant friendship — the verse. A cross-gender pairing in this composition risks reading as romance.

**Portrait orientation pushed again:** Gemini finally gave us portrait on panel 10. We push portrait again here — two seated figures with sky and tree canopy above them is naturally portrait-framable.

## Note before you generate

Five specific failure modes to watch for:

1. **Subject looks skeptical or doubting.** Narrowed eyes, raised eyebrow, pursed mouth, head tilted in disbelief — wrong. The verse is the choice TO believe. Regen note: `the subject's face is calm and warm — not skeptical, not narrowed, not raised-eyebrow. A small soft natural smile, steady engaged eyes, the face of someone who has just chosen to take her friend at her word.`

2. **Subject looks passive or bored.** Disengaged listening isn't believing — it's tolerating. Body must be actively open, leaning in. Regen note: `the subject is leaning slightly forward, body fully turned toward her friend, arms uncrossed, shoulders relaxed and open. Engaged active listening, not passive tolerance.`

3. **Friend looks suspicious or shifty.** If the friend looks like she's lying, the panel flips into "the subject is being naively gullible." Friend must look earnest. Regen note: `the friend's expression is earnest and slightly anxious — the look of someone who has just told the truth about something hard and is waiting to see if her friend will believe her. Open and sincere, not shifty.`

4. **The hand touch reads as comfort instead of agreement.** A hand on a shoulder, on a back, or on an arm reads as bearing-with (panel 10). For this panel the touch must be hand-on-hand, light, resting on the bench between them. Regen note: `the subject's right hand is resting on TOP of her friend's hand on the bench between them. Hand on hand, not shoulder, not arm, not back. Light touch, palm down, the seal of "I believe you."`

5. **Landscape instead of portrait.** Gemini just gave us portrait on panel 10; we want to keep that. Regen note: `the frame must be PORTRAIT orientation — taller than wide. Vertical camera. Tree canopy and sky fill the upper third above the seated figures.`
