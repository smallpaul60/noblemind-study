# Panel 9 — Love does not rejoice in unrighteousness, but rejoices with the truth

**Concept (diptych):** Same Black teen girl in two scenes side by side.

- **LEFT** — School cafeteria lunch table. Three other teens are leaning in, animated and laughing at someone else's expense — gossip or a juicy bad story. The subject is at the table with them but visibly not joining. Hand resting flat on her own lunch tray, face composed, eyes not on the speaker. She is not stepping out of the friend group — she is simply not contributing to the rejoicing.
- **RIGHT** — Church youth-group Bible study circle. The same subject sits with an open Bible in her lap, finger on the verse being read aloud. Her face is lit up — bright eyes, small open smile, slight forward lean. The truth has just landed.

**Why this composition:** Paul wrote v6 as an antithesis — *ou chairei epi tē adikia, synchairei de tē alētheia*, "does not rejoice over unrighteousness, but rejoices together with the truth." The "but" *is* the verse. A diptych is the only honest visualization because the verse defines each half by its opposite. Same teen, two settings, two contents being celebrated — one she refuses, one she delights in.

**Why same subject in both frames:** Like panel 8's Yesterday/Today, the diptych works because the viewer sees *the same person* making the choice in both directions. If the subjects were different, the verse becomes abstract ("some teens do this, other teens do that"). With one teen across both frames, it becomes personal: *this* is what *one Christian teen's life* looks like across her school day and her church night. That is exactly the JH/HS audience's actual experience.

**The physical fix carrier — per [[smallest-physical-fix-first]]:**

- **LEFT:** her hand resting flat on her own lunch tray — grounded in her own meal, not gesturing into the gossip circle. A still hand in a moving group is the refusal.
- **RIGHT:** her finger touching the verse in her open Bible, eyes lit up — she is tracking the text as it is read aloud, and the joy is in the contact with the truth itself.

**No baked-in text labels:** Panel 8 carried "Yesterday" / "Today" / "CONFLICT" baked into the image. For this panel, the contrast is contextual (school vs. church / gossip vs. Scripture) rather than temporal, and the imagery is strong enough to carry it without labels. Cleaner.

**Color grading carries the emotional valence:** Cool / slightly desaturated cafeteria-fluorescent feel on the left (the locked golden-hour palette is deliberately broken for this half), warm golden directional light on the right. This mirrors panel 8's emotional grading and signals the verse contrast at a glance.

## Note before you generate

Five specific failure modes to watch for:

1. **Baked-in text labels.** Panel 8 came back with "Yesterday" / "Today" / "CONFLICT" on the chalkboard. We do not want any labels on this one — the imagery carries the contrast. Regen note: `no baked-in text labels anywhere in either frame. No "School" / "Church", no "Then" / "Now", no chalkboard words. The two scenes carry the contrast on their own.`

2. **The subject on the LEFT looks judgmental or sour.** The verse is "does not rejoice in unrighteousness" — refusal, not condemnation. If she looks scolding, eye-rolling, or visibly disapproving, the panel flips into self-righteousness. Regen note: `her face on the left is composed and quiet — not laughing with the group, not disapproving of them either. She is simply not contributing. Neutral mouth, gaze down or middle-distance, no scowl, no judgment.`

3. **The subject on the RIGHT looks performatively bright.** Staged broad grin, eyes-to-the-sky, hands raised — wrong register. The joy of "rejoicing with the truth" is quiet recognition, not theatrical worship-pose. Regen note: `her face on the right is the quiet bright joy of something true that just landed — bright eyes, a small natural open smile, slight forward lean. Not a staged broad smile, not eyes closed in worship, not hands raised.`

4. **Two different teens appear in the two frames.** The diptych depends on it being the *same girl* in both. If Gemini renders two different Black teen girls, the verse collapses into "some teens do this, other teens do that." Regen note: `the same girl — same face, same hair, same outfit — must appear in both frames. She is one teen photographed in two situations, not two different teens.`

5. **The Bible content is readable / specific.** The Bibles should look like real open Bibles with visible text on the pages, but the text should be soft enough to read as "an open Bible" not as a specific identifiable verse or chapter. Regen note: `the Bible pages are visible as open pages with text, but the specific text is out of focus — no readable verse, no chapter heading visible.`
