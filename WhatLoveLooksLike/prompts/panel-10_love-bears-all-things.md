# Panel 10 — Love bears all things

**Concept:** The front steps of the school building at the end of a hard day. Two teen boys are sitting side by side on the wide concrete step. The friend — a Latino teen boy — is hunched forward, elbows on his knees, head dropped, having a hard moment. He's not crying for the camera — he's just sitting under something heavy. The subject — a biracial teen boy — is sitting close beside him, shoulder-to-shoulder. His right hand is resting steadily on his friend's back between the shoulder blades. He is not looking at his friend's face — he is looking out at the middle distance, present, unhurried, with no urge to fix anything.

**Why this composition:** The Greek *panta stegei* uses an architectural verb — *stegē* is a roof. The verse is about **bearing with** rather than **carrying for**. Love absorbs the hard thing — the friend's bad day, his sin, his weakness, his sadness — without exposing it, without trying to fix it, without making it about the bearer. Per [[smallest-physical-fix-first]], the physical carrier is **the subject's steady hand on his friend's back combined with his outward gaze**. The hand is the covering. The outward gaze is the refusal to probe or stare or perform consolation — he is *with* his friend, not *working on* him.

**Why the subject doesn't look at his friend:** Junior-high and high-school kids in pain don't want eye contact. A friend who locks in on them with concerned eyes wants something — wants them to *talk*, wants them to *get better*, wants the moment to *resolve*. The friend who simply sits next to them with a steady hand and lets them have the moment without performing for it is the one bearing with them. The averted gaze is the gift.

**Why the friend doesn't have to be crying:** The verse covers all kinds of bearing-with — sadness, embarrassment, frustration, family trouble, a failed test, a fight with a parent. We don't specify *why* he's having a hard moment because the verse is the response, not the cause. A hunched posture, head dropped, hands clasped or hanging — these are enough.

**Why same gender:** Two boys on a step, one with a hand on the other's back, is unambiguously the verse — peer friendship absorbing the weight of a moment. A cross-gender pairing in this composition risks reading as romance or as caretaking with a power dynamic.

**Distinct from panel 13 (Love endures all things):** Bears = covers/absorbs the hard thing in this moment, this person. Endures = sustains over time, the long haul (hospital bedside in the plan). Bears is a single sitting; endures is the long watch.

**Portrait orientation emphasized again:** Gemini has gone landscape on every panel since panel 5. We try portrait again here — two seated figures on a step framed vertically with the school building rising behind them is naturally portrait.

## Note before you generate

Five specific failure modes to watch for:

1. **Landscape instead of portrait.** Gemini has defaulted to landscape on every panel since panel 5. We want portrait here — two seated figures on a step framed vertically. Regen note: `the frame must be PORTRAIT orientation — taller than wide. Vertical camera. The school building and sky fill the upper third above the seated figures.`

2. **Subject is looking at his friend's face.** The averted gaze is the verse — concerned eye contact would flip the moment into "trying to fix." Regen note: `the subject is NOT looking at his friend. His gaze is outward, toward the middle distance across the school yard. Calm, present, unhurried — not concerned-face, not probing, just there.`

3. **The hand is patting or rubbing.** A patting hand reads as performative consolation. The hand must be still — resting, staying. Regen note: `the subject's hand is RESTING steadily on his friend's back, between the shoulder blades. Not patting, not rubbing — just present and still. A hand that is staying.`

4. **The friend's face is fully visible and tear-streaked.** This is not a grief scene; this is a bearing-with-a-hard-moment scene. Open dramatic crying flips the register to sentimental. Regen note: `the friend's face is partly hidden by the angle of his head and his hair — he is hunched forward, head dropped. He is not visibly crying. He is simply sitting under something heavy. The viewer cannot fully see his face.`

5. **The pairing reads as romantic.** Two boys on a step with one's hand on the other's back can read as romance if their bodies face each other or if the hand is on the front of the body. Regen note: `both boys are facing forward, side by side on the step, parallel — not angled toward each other. The hand is on the friend's upper back between the shoulder blades, not on his arm or chest or thigh. This is peer friendship — one boy with a steady hand on another boy's back as he bears a hard moment.`
