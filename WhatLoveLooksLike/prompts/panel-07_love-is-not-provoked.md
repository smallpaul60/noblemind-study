# Panel 7 — Love is not provoked

**Concept:** A school hallway between class periods. Two older teen boys are loitering against the lockers along one wall — one of them is calling out toward the subject and beckoning her over with a sarcastic "come here" gesture, the other is smirking and chiming in. The subject — a Latina teen girl — is walking past them down the hallway, gaze forward, stride continuous, face composed. She does not look at them. She is simply walking on.

**Why this composition:** The previous draft (hallway shove already past, boy looking back over his shoulder) defaulted to a romantic-interest read because the model had nothing aggressive *in the frame* — just two teens in the same hallway with one looking back. This version puts the provocation in the present tense: two boys *actively* jeering and beckoning. The visual language of "jeering boys" is unmistakable to the model and to the viewer. Her continued forward motion past them, while the provocation is *currently happening*, is the verse.

**The smallest physical fix carrier:** her **forward-facing eyes** and **continuous stride**. She does not slow, does not turn her head, does not engage. The provocation is hitting her in real time and finding no purchase. Per [[smallest-physical-fix-first]], the verse is in the direction of her face — not in any expression-acrobatics.

**The cowed/fearful trap:** The plan doc specifically flagged this — "strength under pressure, not weakness." If she looks scared, hunched, head-down, or eyes-on-the-floor, the panel flips: she becomes a victim being bullied past. Her posture must be upright. Her stride must be even. Her face must be present — composed, not pinched, not afraid, not performing indifference either. She has decided not to react, and the deciding is over.

**Why mixed-ethnicity jeerers:** Pairing the two boys as one white + one Black peer keeps the moment from reading as racially targeted bullying — which would distract from the verse. This is *peer hallway jerk behavior*, the daily test, not a racial-bullying scene.

## Note before you generate

Four specific failure modes to watch for:

1. **The boys look friendly.** If the model interprets "calling out and waving" as a buddy-greeting, the verse collapses — it becomes "her friends call her over and she'll join in a second." The jeering must read clearly. Regen note: `the two boys are mocking and jeering at her, not greeting her as friends. The beckoning hand is sarcastic — palm up with fingers curling in a "come here" gesture, attached to a smirking, mocking face. Their expressions are unmistakably antagonistic, not friendly.`

2. **She is looking at them.** Forward gaze IS the verse. If her eyes flick toward them at all, she has engaged. Regen note: `her eyes are forward, locked on the hallway ahead — she is not looking at the boys, not glancing sideways, not making eye contact with them.`

3. **She looks scared, hurt, or cowed.** The plan doc flagged this one specifically — "strength under pressure, not weakness." Regen note: `her face is composed and present — not afraid, not flinching, not pinched, not eyes-on-the-floor. The face of someone who has decided not to react. Calm, self-possessed, walking on as she would have anyway.`

4. **She is slowing or stopping.** If her motion is paused, the panel reads as confrontation. She must be in continuous forward motion. Regen note: `she is mid-stride, in continuous forward motion — not stopped, not slowing, not hesitating. Her stride is even and natural.`
