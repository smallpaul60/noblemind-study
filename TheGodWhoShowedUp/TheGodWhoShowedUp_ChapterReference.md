# The God Who Showed Up — Chapter Reference Guide
## Updated March 24, 2026

---

## Chapter Order (Confirmed)

Chapter 1 follows "big picture first" logic (Elohim as Creator). Chapters 2 onward follow chronological order of revelation. Yahweh moves to Chapter 5 as the hinge between the patriarchal era and the Exodus, consistent with Exodus 6:2–3 ("I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as God Almighty, but by My name, Lord, I did not make Myself known to them").

### Introduction: What's In A Name?

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Texts:** Exodus 3:13–14; Genesis 12:1–3; Galatians 3:8; Galatians 3:16; Galatians 3:29; Galatians 6:16; 1 Corinthians 10:1–11; Romans 2:28–29; Romans 9:6–8
**Theme:** The three Abrahamic promises as the driving force behind the story; the shadow framework (Egypt = bondage, Red Sea = deliverance/baptism, wilderness = life of faith, Promised Land = life God calls us into); the Scriptural case for why Israel's journey is the Christian's journey
**Promise Thread:** All three promises introduced — nation (Genesis 12:2), land (Genesis 12:7), blessing to all nations (Genesis 12:3)
**Key Decisions:** Galatians 6:16 handled with honesty — present what we believe, explain why, acknowledge alternative reading of kai, let reader decide. Framework does not depend on that single verse.

### Chapter 1: Elohim — The God Who Was Already There

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Genesis 1:1
**Hebrew:** Elohim — plural form, singular verbs
**Theme:** God exists before anything else. Before the crisis, before the need, before the story of sin begins — He was already there.
**Shadow:** Before your story began, before your crisis, before your need — God was already there.
**Promise Thread:** The God who created everything is the God who will later make promises to Abraham — and He has the authority and power to keep them.
**Cross-References:** John 1:1–3; Colossians 1:16–17; Hebrews 1:10–11
**Further Study Name:** (None — this is the foundation)
**Review Question:** If God was there before the beginning, what does that mean for the things in your life that feel like they have no starting point — no clear origin, no one to blame, no explanation?

### Chapter 2: El Roi — The God Who Sees

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Genesis 16:7–14
**Hebrew:** El Roi — God who sees (ra'ah — attentive, knowing sight)
**Theme:** God sees the invisible. Hagar — a foreign slave woman with no covenant standing — is the first person in Scripture to name God. He sought her; she did not seek Him.
**Shadow:** Every person who has felt unseen, overlooked, forgotten, or invisible needs this name. Nobody is invisible to God.
**Promise Thread:** Hagar's situation arose from Sarai's impatience with the promise. Even the fallout from human failure does not escape God's sight.
**Cross-References:** Genesis 21:8–21 (God sees Hagar again); Exodus 3:7; Psalm 33:13–15; Psalm 139:1–6; 1 Samuel 16:7; 2 Chronicles 16:9; Matthew 6:6
**Further Study Name:** El Olam — The Everlasting God (Genesis 21:33)
**Key Decisions:** Hagar was not calling out to the God of Abram; God sought her. The name Ishmael ("God hears") refers to God giving heed to her affliction, not a prayer she directed at Him. The 'anah root connects Sarai's harsh treatment to Egypt's affliction of Israel.
**Review Question:** If God saw Hagar — a foreign slave with no standing and no claim on His promises — what makes you think He has overlooked you?

### Chapter 3: El Shaddai — God Almighty

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Genesis 17:1–8
**Hebrew:** El Shaddai — God Almighty (meaning derived from usage pattern, not debated etymology)
**Theme:** The God for whom nothing is impossible. Revealed to a 99-year-old man with a barren wife. The name under which the patriarchs knew God (Exodus 6:2–3).
**Shadow:** The exhaustion of waiting — not crisis but settled resignation. God interrupts the settling.
**Promise Thread:** The Abrahamic promises are expanded, formalized, and sealed as an everlasting covenant under this name. Name changes (Abram→Abraham, Sarai→Sarah). God refuses Ishmael as substitute for the promise.
**Cross-References:** Genesis 18:10–14; Genesis 21:1–7; Exodus 6:2–3; Genesis 28:3; Genesis 35:11; Genesis 48:3; Job 42:2; Luke 1:37
**Further Study Name:** El Olam — The Everlasting God (Genesis 21:33, from a different angle than Chapter 2)
**Key Decisions:** Etymology of Shaddai — do not pick a debated root and present it as settled. Let usage pattern define meaning. Every appearance ties to overwhelming power overriding natural impossibility.
**Review Question:** Where in your life have you stopped expecting what God promised and started settling for what seems possible — and what would it mean to hear Him say, "I am El Shaddai"?

### Chapter 4: Jehovah Jireh — The Lord Will Provide

**Status:** Complete (revised March 23, 2026 — seh/ayil section, chapter number, cross-reference)
**Primary Text:** Genesis 22:1–14
**Hebrew:** Yahweh Yireh — the Lord will see/provide (yireh from same root as ra'ah in El Roi)
**Theme:** God's provision is not late, not improvised, already in place. Abraham's faith: he believed God could raise the dead (Hebrews 11:17–19). The future tense of the name — not "provided" but "will provide."
**Shadow:** Costly obedience. The mountain where the command and the promises seem to contradict.
**Promise Thread:** All three promises reaffirmed by oath (Genesis 22:16–18). God swears by Himself (Hebrews 6:13). Moriah = temple mount (2 Chronicles 3:1).
**Cross-References:** Hebrews 11:17–19; Hebrews 6:13–18; 2 Chronicles 3:1; John 1:29; Romans 8:32; Philippians 4:19
**Further Study Name:** El Elyon — God Most High (Genesis 14:18–20)
**Key Decisions:** Seh/ayil distinction — Abraham used the standard word for a sacrifice animal (seh), likely reassuring his son. God provided a ram (ayil). The distinction is real and preserved in the text. John 1:29 calls Jesus the Lamb. The thread is there; Scripture draws it. We present it without pushing the reader toward a prophetic reading of Abraham's words. Lamb vs. ram on Moriah handled with restraint — the question is raised, the reader is trusted.
**Review Question:** What are you holding so tightly that you have not yet placed it on the altar — and what would it look like to trust that the God who provided for Abraham will provide for you?

### Chapter 5: Yahweh (YHWH) — The Self-Existent One

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Exodus 3:13–15
**Hebrew:** Ehyeh asher Ehyeh — "I AM WHO I AM." First person form of hayah (to be). Yahweh is the third person form (He IS). The imperfect form expresses continuous, uncompleted being.
**Theme:** The covenant name. The personal name of God revealed at the moment of deliverance. Self-existent, eternal, dependent on nothing. The hinge between the patriarchal era and the Exodus.
**Shadow:** Egypt as the bondage of sin. God comes down to deliver. The Yahweh who delivered Israel is the God who delivers us.
**Promise Thread:** God identifies Himself as "the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" — the Abrahamic promises are in the room. Exodus 6:2–3 ties Yahweh to El Shaddai as the progression from patriarchal to covenant name.
**Cross-References:** Exodus 6:2–3; Joshua 5:15 (sandals parallel); Psalm 90:2; Psalm 102:25–27; John 8:56–59; Hebrews 13:8; Revelation 1:8
**Further Study Name:** Jehovah Mekoddishkem — The Lord Who Sanctifies You (Exodus 31:13)
**Key Decisions:** Sandals command appears twice (Exodus 3, Joshua 5:15) — both at promise-fulfillment thresholds. Observation raised as a question, not a doctrine. No use of "Tetragrammaton" or other man-made labels. John 8:58 — Jesus claims the burning bush name; the Jewish leaders understood exactly what He said. Seven "I am" statements noted.
**Review Question:** If the God you are praying to is I AM — self-existent, unchanging, dependent on nothing — what does that do to the fear that your situation might be too big for Him?

### Chapter 6: Jehovah Rapha — The Lord Who Heals

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Exodus 15:22–27 (Marah and Elim)
**Hebrew:** Yahweh Ropheka — the Lord your healer. Verb: rapha — to heal, cure, restore, make whole.
**Theme:** God heals what has gone wrong. The first act of rapha is not healing a body but healing water — restoration in the broadest sense. The name is unconditional (God IS the healer), but the specific Marah promise was framed within the covenant relationship.
**Shadow:** Bitterness from disappointment, not disaster. Marah is what happens after the miracle — when the thing you hoped for is not what you expected. God transforms from the inside rather than replacing.
**Promise Thread:** Israel is in the wilderness heading toward the promised land. Every provision along the way — including healing — serves the journey toward the promises.
**Cross-References:** Genesis 20:17; Numbers 12:13; 2 Kings 20:5; 2 Chronicles 7:14; Psalm 103:2–3; Psalm 147:3; Isaiah 53:5; Jeremiah 17:14; James 5:14–16
**Further Study Name:** Jehovah Sabaoth — The Lord of Hosts (1 Samuel 1:3, Isaiah 6:3)
**Key Features:** Opens with the Red Sea crossing given its full weight — the defining act of deliverance, the 1 Corinthians 10:1–2 baptism connection, the Song of Moses. The crossing is the hinge between Egypt and the wilderness. Elim (twelve springs, seventy palms) follows the bitter — after the bitter, abundance.
**Key Decisions:** The tree at Marah — text does not explain what kind or how it worked; we do not invent an explanation. Conditional structure of verse 26 handled honestly — the name is who God is; the specific promise of protection was tied to covenant faithfulness.
**Review Question:** What in your life right now is bitter — the thing that should be sustaining you but is not — and what would it look like to bring it to the God who heals rather than trying to fix it yourself?

### Chapter 7: Jehovah Nissi — The Lord Is My Banner

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Exodus 17:8–16
**Hebrew:** Yahweh Nissi — the Lord is my banner. Nes = banner, standard, rallying point.
**Theme:** The first battle in the wilderness. The victory depends on intercession (hilltop) combined with action (battlefield). Moses cannot hold his arms up alone — Aaron and Hur hold him up. The banner is not what God did but what God IS — the rallying point.
**Shadow:** The enemy in the wilderness attacks the weak, the weary, the stragglers (Deuteronomy 25:17–18). The pattern fits the framework without dogmatic declaration. The war is "from generation to generation" — ongoing, not a one-time event. The Christian life is not a solo campaign.
**Promise Thread:** Israel is heading toward the promised land; the enemy attacks on the road. The war God declares against Amalek persists through the OT narrative (Numbers 14:45; Judges 6:3; 1 Samuel 15; 1 Samuel 30; Esther 3:1).
**Cross-References:** Deuteronomy 25:17–19; 1 Samuel 15:1–35; 1 Samuel 30:1–20; Esther 3:1; Numbers 21:1–3; Isaiah 11:10–12; Ephesians 6:10–18; 2 Corinthians 10:3–5; 1 Timothy 6:12
**Further Study Name:** El Gibbor — Mighty God (Isaiah 9:6)
**Key Decisions:** Amalek as shadow of the enemy in the wilderness — presented as pattern within the established 1 Corinthians 10 framework, not as dogmatic type. Deuteronomy 25:17–18 does the heavy lifting. "From generation to generation" traced through Scripture to show the war is ongoing.
**Review Question:** Where is the enemy attacking you right now — not at your place of strength, but at your place of weariness — and who has God placed beside you to hold up your arms?

### Chapter 8: Jehovah Shalom — The Lord Is Peace

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Judges 6:11–24
**Hebrew:** Yahweh Shalom — the Lord is peace. Shalom = completeness, wholeness, well-being — not just the absence of conflict but the presence of everything right.
**Theme:** God calls us what we are becoming, not what we are. Gideon is hiding in a winepress and God calls him "valiant warrior" (gibbor hayil). The shalom on this altar is not the absence of conflict with Midian — the battle hasn't begun. It is God's answer to the terror of His own holiness: "Peace to you. Do not fear; you shall not die."
**Shadow:** Living in the gap between what God promised and what life looks like. The honest question: "If the Lord is with us, why has this happened?" God does not rebuke the question. He answers by showing up.
**Promise Thread:** Israel is in the land of promise but not experiencing the promise. The oppression is a consequence of the Judges cycle. The Amalekites join Midian (Judges 6:3) — the enemy from Rephidim still showing up, generation after generation. The promises haven't failed; Israel has stopped walking in them. God shows up anyway.
**Cross-References:** Exodus 3:12 ("I will be with you" — same promise to Moses); Exodus 33:20; Judges 6:25–27 (tearing down the altar of Baal); Judges 7:2, 7:12 (300 men vs. locusts); Numbers 6:24–26; Isaiah 26:3; Isaiah 57:19–21; John 14:27; John 16:33; Romans 5:1; Philippians 4:6–7; Colossians 3:15
**Further Study Name:** Sar Shalom — Prince of Peace (Isaiah 9:6)
**Key Decisions:** The angel of the Lord / the Lord shift (vv. 11–12 vs. v. 14) — present what the text shows without man-made labels like "theophany" or "Christophany." Gideon's honest question treated with respect, not rebuke. God's response is a commission, not an explanation — the prophet already gave the diagnosis (6:7–10). Gideon's later victory (300 men, torches, jars) referenced briefly to show "valiant warrior" proved true, but focus stays on the moment the name is given. Connection to El Shaddai chapter — God named Abraham "father of a multitude" before there was a child; God names Gideon "warrior" while he hides in a winepress.
**Review Question:** What has God called you to that you have been hiding from — not because you do not believe in Him, but because you do not believe He could mean you?

### Chapter 9: Jehovah Rohi — The Lord Is My Shepherd

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Psalm 23:1–6
**Hebrew:** Yahweh Rohi — the Lord is my shepherd. Verb ra'ah = to tend, to pasture, to shepherd — full responsibility for living things that cannot care for themselves.
**Theme:** Not a crisis moment but the settled confidence of a man who has been through the crises and knows his Shepherd's character. David was a literal shepherd — fought a lion and a bear (1 Samuel 17:34–36). Every line is informed by lived experience. This name carries all the others within it.
**Shadow:** Rohi does not address a moment — it addresses all of it. The shepherd is not a crisis responder. He is present in the green pastures and in the valley. The Christian life is a flock and a shepherd, not a series of disconnected emergencies.
**Promise Thread:** David is the king in the promised land. The shepherd-king who governs the nation God promised to Abraham. Rohi connects to every provision along the journey.
**Cross-References:** 1 Samuel 17:34–36; Psalm 100:3; Isaiah 40:11; Ezekiel 34:11–16; John 10:1–18; John 10:14–15; John 10:27–28; Hebrews 13:20–21; 1 Peter 2:25; 1 Peter 5:4
**Further Study Name:** Jehovah Raah — alternate transliteration of the same root (ra'ah). Through the Valley (NobleMind Press) referenced as companion volume for Psalm 23:4.
**Key Decisions:** The pronoun shift — vv. 1–3 are third person ("He makes me," "He leads me"), v. 4 shifts to second person ("You are with me," "Your rod"). When it gets darkest, the language gets most intimate. The radaph observation — "follow" in v. 6 is the Hebrew word for "pursue/chase," used elsewhere for enemies in battle. Goodness and lovingkindness are chasing David down. John 10 as the fulfillment — the good shepherd lays down His life, knows His sheep by name, and no one snatches them from His hand. The valley acknowledged as carrying more weight than one chapter can hold — deferred to Through the Valley for full treatment.
**Review Question:** When you read "The Lord is my shepherd" — do you believe the word my? Not as theology. As experience. Do you know His voice? And when did you last hear it?

### Chapter 10: Jehovah Tsidkenu — The Lord Our Righteousness

**Status:** Complete
**Primary Text:** Jeremiah 23:5–6
**Hebrew:** Yahweh Tsidqenu — the Lord our righteousness.
**Theme:** Unlike every other name so far, this one does not describe an action — it describes something God IS and something He GIVES. His righteousness becomes ours. The coming King does not call His people to be righteous enough; He becomes their righteousness.
**Shadow:** The awareness — accurate, not false guilt — that we are not good enough. Tsidkenu does not argue with that. It says the righteousness you need is not yours. It is His. And He gives it.
**Promise Thread:** By Jeremiah's day, the Abrahamic promises seem to be unraveling — exile coming, land about to be lost, nation about to be scattered. But the Branch (tsemach) will grow from the stump. The promises have not failed. What looks like the end is the setup for something greater.
**Cross-References:** Jeremiah 23:11, 14, 21; Jeremiah 33:14–16; Jeremiah 6:14; 2 Kings 24:19; Isaiah 11:1–2; Isaiah 53:11; Zechariah 3:8; Zechariah 6:12–13; Romans 3:21–22; Romans 5:1; Romans 5:17; 2 Corinthians 5:21; Philippians 3:8–9
**Further Study Name:** Attiq Yomin — Ancient of Days (Daniel 7:9). Daniel and Jeremiah as near-contemporaries watching the same exile. Daniel saw the Son of Man approaching the eternal throne. Jeremiah saw the righteous Branch. Both are looking at the same King.
**Key Decisions:** The Zedekiah wordplay — Zedekiah's name (Tsidqiyyahu = "The Lord is my righteousness") vs. the promised King's name (Yahweh Tsidqenu = "The Lord our righteousness"). The irony is in the text: the king whose name claims righteousness is the most unrighteous king on the throne. The shift from "my" to "our" matters — Zedekiah's claim was personal and unsustainable; the coming King's righteousness is communal and given. The Branch (tsemach) traced across the prophets — Isaiah 11:1–2 (shoot from Jesse's stump), Zechariah 6:12. 2 Corinthians 5:21 is the fulfillment: Scripture interpreting Scripture. The connection between Shalom and Tsidkenu — Shalom said "you will not die" but did not explain how; Tsidkenu provides the basis.
**Review Question:** If you knew — truly believed — that your standing before God depended entirely on His righteousness and not on yours, what burden would you set down today?

### Chapter 11: Jehovah Shammah — The Lord Is There

**Status:** Complete (March 24, 2026)
**Primary Text:** Ezekiel 48:35
**Hebrew:** Yahweh Shammah — the Lord is there. Shammah = there. The simplest name in the book — not a compound theological statement, just a location.
**Theme:** The last word of Ezekiel's prophecy is a name. After the glory departs, the bones are dry, the exile seems permanent — the final word is not judgment but presence. He is there.
**Shadow:** Spiritual exile — the distance you feel when you have wandered far from where you know you should be, not because someone carried you away but because you walked away on your own. Shammah answers: He is there.
**Promise Thread:** By Ezekiel's day, all three Abrahamic promises appear to be lost — nation scattered, land conquered, blessing to all nations impossible. And yet: the bones live, the glory returns, the city gets a new name. The promises have never failed. What looks like the end has never been the end.
**Cross-References:** Ezekiel 8:12, 14, 16 (abominations in the temple); Ezekiel 10:4, 18 (glory departing from the temple); Ezekiel 11:22–23 (glory departing from the city toward the east); Ezekiel 37:1–14 (valley of dry bones, "Can these bones live?", God's Spirit within); Ezekiel 43:2, 4–5 (glory returning from the east); Ezekiel 43:7 ("I will dwell among the sons of Israel forever"); Psalm 139:7–12; Revelation 21:3
**Further Study Name:** Abba — Father (Mark 14:36; Romans 8:15; Galatians 4:6). The God who is there is not a distant presence. He is a Father — and the Spirit He has placed within us is the proof that the relationship is real, not formal. The presence Ezekiel saw returning from the east now dwells within every believer.
**Key Decisions:** The departure of the glory handled in detail — stage by stage, toward the east, as if giving the city every chance to turn back. The exile behind the exile: the people drove God's presence from His own house; the physical exile was a symptom, not the cause. Ezekiel's temple vision (chapters 40–48) — debated among faithful students; we do not attempt to settle the discussion; what matters is the return of the glory and God's declaration of permanent dwelling. The dry bones passage given significant space — Ezekiel's answer ("O Lord God, You know") treated as one of the most faithful lines in Scripture. The name's simplicity is the point — after everything, the simplest possible statement carries the most weight.
**Review Question:** If the last word of Ezekiel's prophecy — after forty-eight chapters of judgment, exile, and restoration — is not judgment but presence, what does that tell you about what matters most to God?

### Chapter 12: Immanuel — God With Us

**Status:** Complete (March 24, 2026)
**Primary Text:** Isaiah 7:14 / Matthew 1:22–23
**Hebrew:** Immanu-El — with us + God. The same El from Genesis 1.
**Theme:** Every OT name revealed something about God's character from a distance — through a gap, through a veil. This name closes the distance entirely. Not what God does but where God is: with us. Immanuel is not one more name on the list. It is the name the whole list was building toward.
**Shadow:** The deepest version of the human need is not for any specific provision but for God Himself — for someone to be with you. Immanuel meets the need underneath all the needs.
**Promise Thread:** Fulfillment of the third promise — all nations blessed through Abraham's seed, who is Christ (Galatians 3:16). Every name revealed along the journey was pointing here.
**Cross-References:** Isaiah 7:2, 11, 12 (Ahaz's crisis and refusal); Matthew 1:21 (Jesus/Yeshua — "He will save His people from their sins"); John 1:14 ("dwelt" = tabernacled among us — connects to tabernacle/temple/glory thread); Exodus 40:34; 1 Kings 8:10–11; Colossians 1:19 (all the fullness dwelling in Him); Hebrews 1:1–3 (many portions and many ways, now in His Son — the exact representation); Galatians 3:16; Galatians 4:4; Philippians 2:6–8; Exodus 33:11, 20; Genesis 5:22–24; Genesis 32:24–30; Ezra 3:12; Matthew 6:8; Hebrews 10:22
**Further Study Name:** Alpha and Omega — The Beginning and the End (Revelation 1:8; 21:6; 22:13). The journey from Elohim ("In the beginning, God") to Immanuel (God with us, in the flesh) held together by the one who stands at both ends of the story. Matthew 28:20 — "I am with you always, even to the end of the age."
**Key Decisions:** Opens with Hebrews 1:1–2 and walks back through every name in the book as examples of the "many portions and many ways." The almah/virgin question handled simply — Matthew settles it by how he applies it; Scripture interprets Scripture. Jesus vs. Immanuel: Jesus is the name on the birth record; Immanuel is who He is. No use of "Incarnation" or "Christmas" — both are man-made terms. The four hundred years of prophetic silence noted as context. John 1:14 "dwelt" connected to the tabernacle/temple thread from Shammah. Colossians 1:19 — all the fullness, not a portion. The chapter ends looking forward to the Conclusion — "Let us draw near" (Hebrews 10:22) previewed but deferred to the Conclusion for full treatment.
**Review Question:** If every name in this book was a portion of who God is — revealed one crisis at a time across thousands of years — what does it mean that all of those names now dwell fully in one person, and that person has promised to be with you?

### Conclusion: He Is Still Showing Up

**Status:** Complete (March 24, 2026)
**Primary Texts:** Matthew 27:51; Hebrews 10:19–22; Exodus 3:5; Joshua 5:15; Luke 15:19, 22; Psalm 50:15; Romans 10:13; Romans 8:15; Exodus 33:20
**Theme:** The torn veil — from top to bottom, from God's side reaching down. Every name is now accessible through Jesus directly. The communion God intended in Eden is restored. The sandals-and-sonship shift: sandals off (OT posture before the holy God) to sandals on (the Father restoring the son in Luke 15:22). Connection to A New and Living Way.
**Key Decisions:** The veil as the physical symbol of the barrier since Eden — every name in the book was revealed through that separation. Matthew 27:51: torn from top to bottom, from God's side. Hebrews 10:19–22: the new and living way. The sandals observation: Exodus 3:5 and Joshua 5:15 (sandals off — servant on holy ground) vs. Luke 15:22 (sandals on — son restored). Servants did not wear sandals; sons did. Before the veil: sandals off. After the veil: sandals on — clothed in Tsidkenu's righteousness, welcomed home as a son/daughter, crying "Abba! Father!" (Romans 8:15). A New and Living Way referenced as the companion volume — this book teaches who you are praying to; that one teaches how to pray. The closing recitation of all the names. Final invitation: "Call on Him. He will answer. He always has." Closing Scripture: Romans 10:13.
