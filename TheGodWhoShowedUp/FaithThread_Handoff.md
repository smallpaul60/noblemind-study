## THE PLAIN ANSWER FROM SCRIPTURE

**Romans 10:17** — "So faith comes from hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ."

**The full chain — Romans 10:14-17:**
- God sends preachers (v.15)
- Preachers preach (v.14)
- People hear (v.14)
- Hearing produces faith (v.17)

This is a process that involves human participation at every step. God provides the grace, the word, and the opportunity. Man must hear it, receive it, and respond. This is consistent with the entire biblical pattern.

**Suggested reply:**
"Romans 10:17 — 'So faith comes from hearing, and hearing by the word of Christ.' God sends the message. Man hears it and believes it. The whole chain is in Romans 10:14-17 — sending, preaching, hearing, believing. God provides the grace and the word. Man must hear it, receive it, and respond. That's not faith alone and it's not works alone. It's God and man — exactly the way every relationship works."

## ANTICIPATED FOLLOW-UP AND PREPARED RESPONSES

### If he cites Ephesians 2:8-9 claiming faith is "the gift of God":

**The text:** "For by grace you have been saved through faith; and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God; not as a result of works, so that no one may boast."

**The answer:** The Greek word for "that" (touto — τοῦτο) is neuter. The word "faith" (pistis — πίστις) is feminine. In Greek grammar, a pronoun must agree with its antecedent in gender. Since "that" is neuter and "faith" is feminine, "that" does not refer to faith. It refers to the entire preceding concept — salvation by grace through faith. The gift of God is the whole arrangement, not faith specifically.

**Supporting logic:** If faith were the gift — something God gives irresistibly to the elect — then commanding people to believe would be meaningless. But Scripture commands belief everywhere: "Believe in the Lord Jesus, and you will be saved" (Acts 16:31). You do not command someone to receive something they have no choice in.

### If he goes to John 6:44 — "No one can come to Me unless the Father who sent Me draws him":

**The answer:** The word "draws" (helkuo — ἑλκύω) means to attract or pull toward — not to irresistibly compel. The same word is used in John 12:32: "And I, if I am lifted up from the earth, will draw ALL men to Myself." If "draw" means irresistible election in John 6:44, then John 12:32 teaches universal salvation — because Jesus says He will draw ALL men. The Calvinist cannot have it both ways. God draws all men through the preaching of the Gospel. Man chooses whether to respond.

### If he goes to Romans 9 — "Jacob I loved, Esau I hated" / the potter and the clay:

**The answer:** Romans 9 is about God's sovereign right to choose HOW He works His plan — through which nation, which lineage, which method — not about individual predestination to salvation or damnation. The context is Paul explaining why Israel as a nation has been set aside and Gentiles included. It is about God's freedom in choosing the METHOD, not about God choosing individuals for heaven or hell before they are born. Romans 9:30-32 makes this clear — Israel stumbled because they pursued righteousness by works instead of by faith. That is a CHOICE, not a decree.

### If he goes to Philippians 1:29 — "it has been granted to you... to believe in Him":

**The answer:** "Granted" (charizomai) means "given as a grace/favor" — God graciously provided the opportunity, the message, and the circumstances for belief. It does not mean He implanted belief irresistibly. The Philippians heard the Gospel through Paul's preaching (Acts 16). They chose to believe. God granted the opportunity. Man responded.
