## TITLE: What's In A Name?

---

## OPENING CONCEPT

When someone tells you their name, they are giving you access to who they are. When God tells you His name, He is revealing His character — and inviting you to know Him.

This is not a dictionary of divine titles. This is a journey — the same journey Israel walked out of Egypt and into the promises of God. The same journey every Christian walks today.

---

## THE THREE PROMISES TO ABRAHAM

God made three promises to Abraham that drive the entire biblical narrative. Every name revealed in this book is revealed in the context of God moving His people toward these promises.

1. **"I will make you a great nation"** (Genesis 12:2) — One man, one barren wife, and a promise that defied every natural possibility. Fulfilled through Israel's growth from a family to a nation no one could number.

2. **"To your descendants I will give this land"** (Genesis 12:7) — The land promise that drove the entire journey from Egypt through the wilderness and into Canaan. Every name God revealed in the wilderness was revealed on the road to that promise.

3. **"In you all the families of the earth will be blessed"** (Genesis 12:3) — The promise that reaches across the testaments and lands on us. Paul calls it the Gospel preached in advance: "The Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, 'All the nations will be blessed in you'" (Galatians 3:8).

The promises are the reason for the journey. The names are revealed along the journey. And the fulfillment of the third promise — all nations blessed — is the reason we are reading this book today.

---

## THE SHADOW FRAMEWORK

Israel's journey is not just ancient history. It is the Christian's journey.

- **Egypt** = the world, the bondage of sin
- **Pharaoh** = the power that enslaves
- **The Red Sea** = deliverance, baptism (1 Corinthians 10:1-2)
- **The Wilderness** = the life of faith, where God reveals Himself through what He provides
- **The Promised Land** = the life God calls us into

Paul explicitly teaches this in **1 Corinthians 10:1-11** — "Now these things happened to them as an example, and they were written for our instruction." This is not a framework we are imposing on the text. The text establishes it.

---

## WHY THIS JOURNEY IS OURS — THE SCRIPTURAL CASE

Multiple passages establish that Christians are heirs of the Abrahamic promises and participants in Israel's story:

### Clear and undisputed texts:

**1 Corinthians 10:1-11** — Israel's experiences in the wilderness happened as examples and were written for our instruction. Paul applies their story directly to the Christian.

**Galatians 3:8** — "The Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, 'All the nations will be blessed in you.'" The promise to Abraham was the Gospel announced in advance.

**Galatians 3:29** — "And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's descendants, heirs according to promise." Christians are Abraham's descendants. The promises made to Abraham are our inheritance.

**Galatians 3:16** — "Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed. He does not say, 'And to seeds,' as referring to many, but rather to one, 'And to your seed,' that is, Christ." The seed of Abraham through whom all nations are blessed is Christ Himself.

**Romans 2:28-29** — "For he is not a Jew who is one outwardly, nor is circumcision that which is outward in the flesh. But he is a Jew who is one inwardly; and circumcision is that which is of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the letter." Paul redefines "Jew" in terms of the heart, not ethnicity.

**Romans 9:6-8** — "For they are not all Israel who are descended from Israel; nor are they all children because they are Abraham's descendants, but: 'through Isaac your descendants will be named.' That is, it is not the children of the flesh who are children of God, but the children of the promise are regarded as descendants." Paul explicitly distinguishes between ethnic Israel and true Israel — the children of promise.

### Galatians 6:16 — "The Israel of God" (handle with honesty)

**The text:** "And those who will walk by this rule, peace and mercy be upon them, and upon the Israel of God."

**What we believe this text is saying:** Paul is identifying Christians — those who walk by the rule of new creation (v.15) — as the Israel of God. In a letter that has spent six chapters demolishing the wall between Jew and Gentile in Christ (3:28 — "there is neither Jew nor Greek"), Paul's closing benediction applies the title "Israel of God" to the new creation people of God.

**Why we believe this:**
- Galatians 6:15, the verse immediately before, says "neither circumcision nor uncircumcision means anything, but a new creation" — Paul has just erased the ethnic distinction. It would be contradictory to immediately re-establish it in the next verse.
- The entire argument of Galatians has been that belonging to Christ — not ethnic descent or circumcision — is what makes someone an heir of Abraham's promises (3:7, 3:29).
- Paul uses similar logic in Romans 2:28-29 and Romans 9:6-8 to redefine who the true people of God are.

**The honest acknowledgment:** Some faithful students of Scripture read the Greek word kai ("and") in this verse as connecting two separate groups — Christians generally, and believing Jews specifically — rather than identifying them as the same group. This reading takes kai in its most common sense as simply "and." The phrase "Israel of God" appears nowhere else in Paul's writings, so we cannot cross-reference it directly.

**Our position and why:** While the debate is real, the weight of Galatians as a whole leans strongly toward identifying Christians as the Israel of God. Paul has spent the entire letter arguing that faith in Christ — not ethnicity or law-keeping — defines God's people. A sudden return to ethnic distinction in his closing benediction would undermine his own argument. We believe the context of the letter, the immediate context of verse 15, and the supporting evidence from Romans settle the matter — but we present this honestly and let the reader weigh the evidence.

**Critical note for writing:** Present this passage with integrity. State what we believe, explain why from the text, acknowledge the alternative reading, and let the reader decide. This is consistent with our Bible Study Principles: "Acknowledge when a passage is genuinely difficult or when faithful students have disagreed. Never twist Scripture to fit a predetermined conclusion."

**Essential point:** Even if a reader disagrees on Galatians 6:16, the framework does not depend on that single verse. It stands firmly on 1 Corinthians 10:1-11, Galatians 3:8, Galatians 3:29, Romans 2:28-29, and Romans 9:6-8 — none of which are disputed in this way.

## KEY TEXTS FOR THE INTRODUCTION

- **Galatians 3:8** — The Gospel preached beforehand to Abraham

- **Galatians 6:16** — Christians as "the Israel of God" (present honestly — see notes above)
- **1 Corinthians 10:1-11** — Israel's story as our example and instruction

- **Romans 9:6-8** — Not all descended from Israel are Israel; children of promise are the descendants

## TONE FOR THE INTRODUCTION

The Introduction should:
- Welcome the reader into a journey, not a textbook
- Establish the Abrahamic promises as the driving force behind the story
- Lay out the shadow framework with the Scriptural support — let the text make the case
- Handle Galatians 6:16 with honesty and transparency
- Close with anticipation: we are about to walk with Israel from creation to Immanuel, and every step reveals something about the God who keeps showing up

The Introduction is not the place for all the exegetical detail above. It should be warm, accessible, and compelling. But every claim it makes must be grounded in the texts listed here.
