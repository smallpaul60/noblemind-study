## CORE THESIS

God did not reveal all His names at once. He revealed each one at a specific moment of crisis, need, or covenant — when His people needed to know that particular truth about who He is. Every name answers a question His people were asking, whether they spoke it aloud or not. And every name He revealed then is a name He still answers to now.

---

## FRAMEWORK: THE SHADOW TYPOLOGY

Israel's journey — bondage in Egypt, deliverance, wilderness wandering, provision, battle, and the promised land — is not just ancient history. It is the journey every Christian walks. And it is all driven by the promises God made to Abraham.

God promised Abraham a nation, a land, and a blessing that would reach all nations. Everything that follows in the Old Testament — the slavery in Egypt, the deliverance, the wilderness, the conquest, the exile, and the restoration — is God keeping those promises against every obstacle. And every name He reveals along the way tells us something about the God who keeps His word.

- **Egypt** = the world, the bondage of sin
- **Pharaoh** = the power that enslaves
- **The Red Sea** = deliverance, baptism (1 Corinthians 10:1-2)
- **The Wilderness** = the life of faith, where God reveals Himself through what He provides
- **The Promised Land** = the life God calls us into
- **The Abrahamic Promises** = the driving force behind the entire journey — and the reason it matters to us today (Galatians 3:8, 3:29, 6:16)

Paul explicitly teaches this framework in **1 Corinthians 10:1-11** — "Now these things happened to them as an example, and they were written for our instruction." The book does not impose this typology on the text. The text establishes it.

And in **Galatians 3:29**, Paul closes the loop: "And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham's descendants, heirs according to promise." The promises made to Abraham are not ancient history. They are our inheritance. The journey Israel walked is the journey we walk. And the God who showed up for them is the God who shows up for us.

Paul makes this identity explicit in **Galatians 6:16**: "And those who will walk by this rule, peace and mercy be upon them, and upon the Israel of God." Christians are not spectators of Israel's story. We are the Israel of God. Their journey is our journey. Their God is our God. The names He revealed to them are the names He still answers to when we call.

Each name of God was revealed somewhere along this journey. As the reader walks with Israel through the story, they discover God's character the same way Israel did: one crisis at a time, one promise at a time.

## WHAT DISTINGUISHES THIS BOOK

1. **Progressive revelation as narrative arc.** The names are arranged in the order God revealed them, telling a story rather than serving as a dictionary or reference guide. The book has a beginning, middle, and end.

2. **The shadow framework.** Egypt as the world, the wilderness as the Christian life. What Israel needed, we need. The names God revealed to them, He reveals to us. Scripture itself establishes this connection — we simply follow it.

3. **No denominational steering wheel.** No Reformed lens, no charismatic lens, no Catholic lens. Just the text, the context, the cross-references, and the question: what did they need, and what does that tell us about who God is?

4. **Prayer connection.** Each chapter closes with a "Praying His Name" section that shows the reader how that specific name shapes their prayer life — connecting directly to A New and Living Way without repeating it.

5. **The NobleMind hermeneutic.** Scripture interprets Scripture. Let clear passages illuminate difficult ones. Ask "What does the text actually say?" before asking "What does it mean?"

6. **The Abrahamic promises as a running thread.** The three promises God made to Abraham — a great nation (Genesis 12:2), a land (Genesis 12:7), and a blessing to all nations (Genesis 12:3) — are the driving force behind the entire biblical narrative. Every name God reveals along the journey is revealed in the context of moving His people toward those promises. The book weaves this thread through each chapter, showing that the names are not isolated revelations but markers on the road from promise to fulfillment. Jireh on the mountain where Abraham was told to sacrifice the son of promise. El Shaddai when the promise seemed impossible. Rapha and Nissi and Shalom in the wilderness on the way to the land of promise. Tsidkenu and Shammah when the exile threatened to erase the promises entirely. And Immanuel as the fulfillment of the third promise — all nations blessed through Abraham's seed, who is Christ (Galatians 3:16). The reader doesn't just learn God's names. They see the big picture — how those names connect to the promises, how the promises connect to Christ, and how Christ connects those promises to us (Galatians 3:29).

## CHAPTER STRUCTURE (Each Chapter)

Each chapter follows a consistent rhythm:

1. **Opening hook** — Place the reader in the moment. What was happening? What did God's people face? What did they need?
2. **The text** — Walk through the passage carefully. Context, original audience, circumstances.
3. **The name revealed** — What name did God reveal? What is the Hebrew? What does it mean — not just linguistically, but relationally?
4. **Cross-references** — Where else does this attribute of God appear in Scripture? How does the rest of the Bible confirm and deepen what this name reveals?
5. **The shadow applied** — Egypt was their world. What is ours? Their crisis is our crisis. What they needed, we need. How does this name speak to the Christian today?
6. **Praying His Name** — How does knowing this name change the way we talk to God? Not a scripted prayer — a connection. You now know who He revealed Himself to be. Talk to Him like it.
7. **For Further Study** — Additional passages for the reader who wants to go deeper, including variations of the name and related attributes.
8. **One Question to Sit With** — A single reflective question.
9. **One Thing to Do** — A practical step.
10. **Closing Scripture block quote** — Gold left-border accent, consistent with all NobleMind books.

## PROPOSED CHAPTER OUTLINE

### Introduction: What's In A Name?

When someone tells you their name, they are giving you access to who they are. When God tells you His name, He is revealing His character — and inviting you to know Him.

This book follows the names of God in the order He revealed them — not as a dictionary, but as a journey. The same journey Israel walked out of Egypt and into the promises of God. The same journey every Christian walks today.

And those promises are specific. God made three promises to Abraham that drive the entire biblical narrative:

1. **"I will make you a great nation"** (Genesis 12:2) — One man, one barren wife, and a promise that defied every natural possibility. Fulfilled through Israel's growth from a family to a nation no one could number.
2. **"To your descendants I will give this land"** (Genesis 12:7) — The land promise that drove the entire journey from Egypt through the wilderness and into Canaan. Every name God revealed in the wilderness was revealed on the road to that promise.
3. **"In you all the families of the earth will be blessed"** (Genesis 12:3) — The promise that reaches across the testaments and lands on us. Paul calls it the Gospel preached in advance: "The Scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, 'All the nations will be blessed in you'" (Galatians 3:8).

Every name in this book is revealed in the context of God moving His people toward those promises. The promises are the reason for the journey. The names are revealed along the journey. And the fulfillment of the third promise — all nations blessed — is the reason we are reading this book today.

Key texts: **Exodus 3:13-14** — "What is His name?" / "I AM WHO I AM"
**Genesis 12:1-3** — The three promises to Abraham
**Galatians 3:8** — The Gospel preached beforehand to Abraham
**Galatians 6:16** — Christians identified as "the Israel of God"

### Chapter 1: Elohim — The God Who Was Already There
**Genesis 1:1** — "In the beginning, God (Elohim) created the heavens and the earth."

- The very first name. Before anything existed, He was already there.
- Elohim is plural in form — used with singular verbs. The very grammar of the Hebrew points to something about God's nature that would not be fully revealed for centuries.
- No crisis here — just creation. The reader meets God before the story of sin begins. He is the starting point.
- **Shadow:** Before your story began, before your crisis, before your need — God was already there.
- **Praying His Name:** When you don't know where to start, start with the God who was there before the beginning.

---

### Chapter 2: Yahweh (YHWH) — The Self-Existent One
**Exodus 3:13-15** — "I AM WHO I AM" — The burning bush

- Moses asks for a name. God gives him the name above all names.
- Not "I was" or "I will be" — "I AM." Present tense. Eternal. Self-existent. Dependent on nothing and no one.
- This is the covenant name — the personal name of God revealed to His people at the moment of their deliverance.
- **Shadow:** The God who called Moses to deliver Israel from bondage is the God who delivers us from the bondage of sin. The name He gave at the burning bush is the name He still answers to.
- **Praying His Name:** You are not praying to a concept. You are praying to I AM — the God who exists right now, in this moment, fully present.

---

### Chapter 3: El Roi — The God Who Sees
**Genesis 16:7-14** — Hagar in the wilderness

- A slave woman. Pregnant. Alone. Cast out. Not part of the covenant. By every human measure, invisible.
- And God shows up. She names Him: "You are a God who sees."
- She is the first person in Scripture to give God a name — and she is a foreign slave woman. That matters.
- **Shadow:** Every person who has felt unseen, overlooked, forgotten, or invisible needs to hear this name. Nobody is invisible to God.
- **Praying His Name:** When you feel like no one sees you, no one knows what you're carrying, no one understands — pray to El Roi. He saw Hagar in the wilderness, and He sees you.

---

### Chapter 4: Jehovah Jireh — The Lord Will Provide
**Genesis 22:1-14** — Abraham, Isaac, and the ram

- The mountain. The knife. The impossible command. And at the last moment — provision.
- Abraham names the place: "The Lord Will Provide."
- Not "the Lord provided" (past tense) or "the Lord is providing" (present). "The Lord WILL provide" — future tense. A statement of faith for every trial that hasn't happened yet.
- **Shadow:** Every Christian will face a mountain where obedience seems to cost everything. Jireh is the name that says: trust Him. He sees what you cannot, and He provides what you do not have.
- **Praying His Name:** When you cannot see how the need will be met, when obedience feels costly, pray to Jehovah Jireh. The God who provided the ram provides still.

---

### Chapter 5: El Shaddai — God Almighty
**Genesis 17:1-8** — Abram at 99, still childless

- God appears to a 99-year-old man with a barren wife and says: "I am God Almighty. Walk before Me and be blameless."
- Then He changes Abram's name to Abraham — father of a multitude. Before there is a single child.
- El Shaddai — the God for whom nothing is too hard. The God of the impossible.
- **Shadow:** When the promise seems dead, when the years have passed, when the math doesn't work — El Shaddai is the name that says: My power is not limited by your circumstances.
- **Praying His Name:** When you have waited so long that hope feels foolish, pray to El Shaddai. He specializes in the impossible.

---

### Chapter 6: Jehovah Rapha — The Lord Who Heals
**Exodus 15:22-27** — The bitter waters of Marah

- Three days out of Egypt. No water. They finally find water and it's bitter. The grumbling begins immediately.
- God shows Moses a tree. Moses throws it in the water. The water becomes sweet.
- Then God reveals the name: "I am the Lord who heals you."
- **Shadow:** The bitterness of the world — disappointment, pain, loss — is real. But God does not leave us in it. He heals. Not always the way we expect, but always.
- **Praying His Name:** When life turns bitter — when the thing you hoped for is not what you found — pray to Jehovah Rapha. He makes bitter waters sweet.

---

### Chapter 7: Jehovah Nissi — The Lord Is My Banner
**Exodus 17:8-16** — The battle with Amalek

- Israel's first battle after leaving Egypt. Moses holds up his hands and Israel prevails. His hands drop and Amalek prevails.
- Aaron and Hur hold up his hands. Israel wins.
- Moses builds an altar and names it: "The Lord Is My Banner."
- **Shadow:** The victory is never ours alone. The battle is the Lord's. And He often uses the people around us to hold us up when we cannot stand on our own.
- **Praying His Name:** When the battle is real and your arms are tired, pray to Jehovah Nissi. He is your banner — and He sends people to hold you up.

---

### Chapter 8: Jehovah Shalom — The Lord Is Peace
**Judges 6:11-24** — Gideon and the angel of the Lord

- Israel is oppressed by Midian. Gideon is hiding in a winepress, threshing wheat in secret because he's afraid.
- The angel of the Lord appears and calls him "mighty warrior" — which he clearly is not. Yet.
- Gideon is terrified that he has seen God and will die. God says: "Peace to you. Do not fear; you shall not die."
- Gideon builds an altar: "The Lord Is Peace."
- **Shadow:** God calls us what we are becoming, not what we are. And when we are afraid, His name is Peace — not the absence of conflict, but the presence of God in the middle of it.
- **Praying His Name:** When fear has you hiding, when you feel unqualified, when the enemy feels bigger than you — pray to Jehovah Shalom. Peace is not a feeling. It's a Person.

---

### Chapter 9: Jehovah Rohi — The Lord Is My Shepherd
**Psalm 23:1-6** — David's declaration

- Written by a man who was himself a shepherd and who knew exactly what the metaphor meant.
- A shepherd provides, protects, guides, corrects, and stays with the sheep — even through the valley of the shadow of death.
- This name is personal: "The Lord is MY shepherd." Not Israel's shepherd in general. Mine.
- **Shadow:** Every Christian is a sheep — dependent, prone to wander, in need of a guide. Rohi is the name that says: He is not managing you from a distance. He is walking with you.
- **Praying His Name:** When you don't know where to go, when the path is dark, when you feel lost — pray to Jehovah Rohi. He leads. You follow. That's enough.

---

### Chapter 10: Jehovah Tsidkenu — The Lord Our Righteousness
**Jeremiah 23:5-6** — The righteous Branch

- Spoken through Jeremiah during one of Israel's darkest periods — corrupt kings, false prophets, impending exile.
- God promises a coming King who will reign wisely and execute justice — and His name will be "The Lord Our Righteousness."
- Not our own righteousness. His. Given to us.
- **Shadow:** Every attempt to stand before God on our own merit fails. This name points forward to Christ, who became our righteousness (2 Corinthians 5:21).
- **Praying His Name:** When your failures feel disqualifying, when guilt says you can't approach God, pray to Jehovah Tsidkenu. His righteousness is what qualifies you — not yours.

---

### Chapter 11: Jehovah Shammah — The Lord Is There
**Ezekiel 48:35** — The last words of Ezekiel's vision

- After 48 chapters of judgment, exile, dry bones, and restoration — the final word of Ezekiel's prophecy is a name.
- The name of the city shall be: "The Lord Is There."
- After everything — the sin, the exile, the destruction of the temple — God's final word is: I am still here.
- **Shadow:** No matter how far you have wandered, no matter how much has been lost, no matter how long the exile — He is there. That is the last word. Not judgment. Presence.
- **Praying His Name:** When you feel like you've gone too far, when the exile feels permanent, when you wonder if God has left — pray to Jehovah Shammah. He is there. He has always been there.

---

### Chapter 12: Immanuel — God With Us
**Isaiah 7:14 / Matthew 1:22-23** — The name that bridges the testaments

- Promised by Isaiah to a faithless king. Fulfilled in a manger in Bethlehem.
- Every name in the Old Testament revealed something about God's character from a distance — His power, His provision, His peace, His healing. This name closes the distance entirely.
- Immanuel does not mean "God sees us" or "God provides for us." It means "God WITH us." He came.
- **Shadow:** The entire Old Testament points to this moment. Every name was a preview. Immanuel is the fulfillment — God did not just show up in a crisis. He moved in permanently.
- **Praying His Name:** You are never praying across a distance. Immanuel means He is here — with you, in you, beside you. Every name you have learned in this book is now as close as your next breath.

---

### Conclusion: He Is Still Showing Up

From Elohim in Genesis 1 to Immanuel in Matthew 1 — every name, every revelation, every crisis met and answered — it is all one story. The story of a God who refuses to be distant. Who reveals Himself not in theological abstractions but in the real, raw, desperate moments of human life.

He showed up for Hagar. For Abraham. For Moses. For Gideon. For David. For Jeremiah. For Ezekiel. And in the fullness of time, He showed up in person — wrapped in human flesh, lying in a manger, bearing the name Immanuel.

But the story does not end in the manger. It ends at the cross — and at what happened the moment He died.

**Matthew 27:51** — "And behold, the veil of the temple was torn in two from top to bottom."

From top to bottom. Not from man's side reaching up, but from God's side reaching down.

Every name in this book was revealed through a separation. After Eden, the open, unhindered communion between God and man was broken. God did not go silent — He walked with Enoch (Genesis 5:22-24), spoke with Moses face to face as a man speaks to his friend (Exodus 33:11), and revealed Himself through burning bushes, pillars of fire, and theophanies. But even Moses was told "you cannot see My face, for no man can see Me and live" (Exodus 33:20). The communion was real. The access was limited. El Roi saw Hagar, but from heaven. Jireh provided, but through a ram. Shammah promised presence, but the temple still had a veil — a thick curtain that said "this far and no further." Only one man, one day a year, could pass through it, and even he had to bring blood.

The veil represented the separation that sin had caused. And when Jesus died, God tore it in two. Not repaired. Not thinned. Torn. The barrier between God and His people was removed — permanently, completely, and by God's own hand.

**Hebrews 10:19-22** — "Therefore, brethren, since we have confidence to enter the holy place by the blood of Jesus, by a new and living way which He inaugurated for us through the veil, that is, His flesh, and since we have a great priest over the house of God, let us draw near with a sincere heart in full assurance of faith."

A new and living way. Through the veil. Through His flesh. Draw near.

This is what every name in this book was building toward. Not just a God who sees, provides, heals, fights, gives peace, shepherds, and promises His presence — but a God who tore down the wall so that the communion He originally intended in Eden could be restored. Every name is now accessible not through a priest, not through a temple, not through a veil, but through Jesus directly.

He is still showing up. And because of the torn veil, we can now show up too — with confidence, with access, and with the full assurance that every name He has ever revealed is as close as our next prayer.

Call on Him. He will answer. He always has.

Key texts: **Matthew 27:51** — The veil torn from top to bottom
**Hebrews 10:19-22** — The new and living way through the veil
**Psalm 50:15** — "Call upon Me in the day of trouble; I shall rescue you, and you will honor Me."
**Romans 10:13** — "Whoever will call on the name of the Lord will be saved."
