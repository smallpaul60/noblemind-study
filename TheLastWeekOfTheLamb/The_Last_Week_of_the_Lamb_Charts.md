## Chart A: "The Thread"

**Placement:** After the Prologue

**Purpose:** Traces the prophetic line from the first promise to the Passover pattern. One thread, one page.

```
Genesis 3:15 — The seed of the woman will crush the serpent's head
        │
Abraham — "In your seed all the nations shall be blessed" (Genesis 22:18)
        │
Judah — "The scepter shall not depart" (Genesis 49:10)
        │
David — "I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever" (2 Samuel 7:12–16)
        │
Bethlehem — "Too small to be counted… yet from you One will go forth" (Micah 5:2)
        │
Isaiah — "Like a lamb that is led to slaughter" (Isaiah 53:7)
        │
Exodus 12 — The Passover Lamb: selected, kept, killed, blood applied
        │
John 1:29 — "Behold, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!"
```

**Design note:** Vertical thread line connecting each node. Simple. The reader should be able to trace the line with a finger from top to bottom and see the entire story in ten seconds.

**Placement:** Calendar Interlude

**Purpose:** Shows how a high-day Sabbath and weekly Sabbath can fall in the same week with a working day between them.

## Chart 8: "The Timing Question"

**Placement:** Chapter 7

**Purpose:** Shows the Synoptic-vs.-John tension on the Passover timing and how both readings work.

| | The Synoptics Say | John Says |
|---|-------------------|-----------|
| **The Last Supper** | A Passover meal — "I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer" (Luke 22:15) | Judas left to betray Him during the meal (John 13:21–30) |
| **The next morning** | Jesus already under arrest and on trial | The Jewish leaders "did not enter into the Praetorium so that they would not be defiled, but might eat the Passover" (John 18:28) |
| **The crucifixion day** | "The day of preparation" (Mark 15:42) | "The day of preparation for the Passover" (John 19:14) |
| **The tension** | Jesus ate the Passover the night before | The leaders had not yet eaten it the next morning |

**Two possible readings:**

| Reading | Explanation | Strength | Limitation |
|---------|-------------|----------|------------|
| **"Passover" = broader festival** | "Eat the Passover" in John 18:28 refers to festival meals, not the lamb specifically. Luke 22:1 shows the terms overlapped. | Simple resolution | Requires "eat the Passover" to mean something other than its most natural sense |
| **Jesus ate early** | Jesus ate the Passover ahead of the national observance because He would be dead before the nation sat down for it. He could not eat the Passover and BE the Passover at the same time. | Preserves natural meaning of both accounts; aligns with Exodus 12 pattern | Inference — the text does not state this in so many words |

**Design note:** Two-part chart. Top half shows the tension. Bottom half shows the two readings side by side with honest strengths and limitations.

## Chart 12: "The Spice Paradox"

**Placement:** Chapter 10

**Purpose:** Shows that Luke and Mark describe events on two different sides of a Sabbath — requiring two Sabbaths.

| Event | Scripture | When It Happened |
|-------|-----------|-----------------|
| Women observe where the body is laid | Luke 23:55 | Wednesday before sundown |
| Women return home and prepare spices and perfumes | Luke 23:56a | ??? |
| Women rest on the Sabbath according to the commandment | Luke 23:56b | A Sabbath |
| **"When the Sabbath was over,"** Mary Magdalene, Mary, and Salome **buy** spices | Mark 16:1 | After a Sabbath ended |
| Women come to the tomb at dawn | Mark 16:2 | Sunday morning |

**The problem:** You cannot prepare what you have not yet purchased.

If there is only one Sabbath (Saturday), then Luke says they prepared spices *before* Saturday, and Mark says they *bought* spices *after* a Sabbath. They cannot prepare spices they haven't bought yet — unless there are **two Sabbaths**: a high-day Sabbath (Thursday), after which they buy spices on Friday, prepare them, and then rest again on the weekly Sabbath (Saturday).

**The solution:** Two Sabbaths with a working day between them.

```
Wed (burial) → Thu (HIGH-DAY SABBATH) → Fri (buy & prepare spices) → Sat (WEEKLY SABBATH) → Sun (tomb empty)
```

**Design note:** The "you cannot prepare what you have not yet purchased" line is the centerpiece. Make it prominent.

## Chart 14: "Friday vs. Wednesday"

**Placement:** Chapter 10

**Purpose:** Side-by-side comparison of the two models.

| Evidence | Friday Crucifixion | Wednesday Crucifixion |
|----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Sign of Jonah (Matt 12:40)** | 2 nights (Fri, Sat), partial days — requires "three days and three nights" to be figurative | 3 nights (Wed, Thu, Fri) + 3 days (Thu, Fri, Sat) — literal fulfillment |
| **"High day" Sabbath (John 19:31)** | Must mean the weekly Saturday Sabbath | Nisan 15 = feast-day Sabbath (Lev 23:6–7) — a different Sabbath than Saturday |
| **The spice sequence** | Women buy AND prepare spices on Saturday evening after one Sabbath — very compressed timeline | Women buy spices Friday (after high-day Sabbath), prepare them, rest again Saturday (weekly Sabbath) — two Sabbaths, one working day |
| **"Preparation day"** | Friday — the day before the weekly Sabbath | The day before any Sabbath — in this case, the high-day Sabbath of Nisan 15 |
| **Emmaus: "third day" (Luke 24:21)** | Counting from Friday: Sat=1, Sun=2 — only the second day | Counting from Thursday (guard posted, last public event): Fri=1, Sat=2, Sun=3 — fits |
| **Passover typology** | Nisan 14 on a Friday — no alignment with Nisan 10 entry | Nisan 14 on a Wednesday — entry on Nisan 10 (Saturday), four-day keeping period aligns with Exodus 12 |

**Design note:** Two columns, point by point. Let the comparison speak for itself.
