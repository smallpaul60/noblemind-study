## Understanding the Hebrew Calendar — Interlude

**STATUS: DRAFTED (standalone study exists)**

**Source material:** Understanding the Hebrew Calendar (md)

Short section (3–5 pages) placed before Part Two. The reader needs this context before encountering Nisan dates and sundown transitions.

- Days begin at sundown (Genesis 1:5 — "there was evening and there was morning, one day")
  - An evening meal belongs to the NEXT day, not the day ending
  - Example: Tuesday evening after sundown = beginning of Wednesday
- Hebrew months are lunar — new month begins with the new crescent moon
  - Nisan = first month of the religious year (March/April)
  - Nisan 14 = Passover
- Two kinds of Sabbath:
  - Weekly Sabbath = every Saturday (7th day)
  - Feast-day Sabbath = "high day" — commanded rest on specific feast dates regardless of what day of the week they fall on
  - Nisan 15 (first day of Unleavened Bread) = a feast-day Sabbath (Leviticus 23:6–7)
  - These two kinds of Sabbath can fall on different days in the same week
- Why this matters: the word "Sabbath" in the Gospel accounts does not always mean Saturday

**Key Scriptures:** Genesis 1:5; Leviticus 23:4–8; John 19:31

Color-coded. Simple and clear. This is the visual the reader needs to hold in their mind before entering the week-by-week narrative.

## Part Two — The Week

### Chapter 3: The Arrival and the Selection — Nisan 9–10

**STATUS: NOT STARTED**

**Source material:** The Last Week of the Lamb Timeline (Parts Two, Three, Four), From Bethany to the Empty Tomb chart

- John 12:1 — "six days before the Passover" — Jesus arrives in Bethany
  - Inclusive counting (standard Jewish reckoning) — explained and demonstrated
  - Counting backward from Nisan 14: Day 1=14, Day 2=13, Day 3=12, Day 4=11, Day 5=10, Day 6=9
  - Arrival = Friday, Nisan 9
- Supper at Lazarus's house — Friday evening = beginning of Nisan 10 (Sabbath)
  - Mary anoints His feet with costly perfume (John 12:2–8)
  - Sabbath meal — Martha serving is not a violation
- John 12:12 — "On the next day" — the entry into Jerusalem
  - Next day after Friday arrival = Saturday, Nisan 10
  - Crowds spread garments and palm branches
  - "Hosanna to the Son of David!"
- Mark 11:11 — Jesus enters temple, looks around, returns to Bethany. Remarkably restrained.
  - No teaching. No confrontation. No cleansing. Just observation.
  - This restraint makes sense on the Sabbath
  - The action begins "the next day" (Mark 11:12)
- The Sabbath entry — addressed honestly:
  - Distance within a Sabbath day's journey (Acts 1:12)
  - Riding a donkey not considered labor
  - Pharisees objected to messianic praise (Luke 19:39), NOT to Sabbath violation — significant silence
  - The crowd's actions were their own
- Independent confirmation: Mark's "next day" sequence traced backward from Wednesday also lands on Saturday for the entry
- **The Exodus 12 parallel:** Nisan 10 = the day the Passover lamb was to be selected (Exodus 12:3). Jesus enters Jerusalem — presented to the nation — on the exact day God appointed for the lamb to be chosen. Noted honestly as typological inference, not explicit Gospel statement.
- Testing the alternative: exclusive counting puts the entry on Friday, which places the temple cleansing on the Sabbath — merchants wouldn't be operating. The inclusive count avoids this problem entirely.

**Key Scriptures:** John 12:1–19; Mark 11:1–11; Luke 19:28–44; Exodus 12:3; Acts 1:12

**STATUS: NOT STARTED**

**Source material:** The Lamb and the Passover (John timing study), Book Outline notes

- Tuesday evening — sundown = beginning of Nisan 14 in Hebrew reckoning
- The Last Supper preparation (Matthew 26:17–19; Mark 14:12–16; Luke 22:7–13)
  - "Where do You want us to prepare for You to eat the Passover?"
  - A man carrying a jar of water — specific instructions
- The meal itself:
  - "I have earnestly desired to eat this Passover with you before I suffer" (Luke 22:15)

  - The cup: "This cup which is poured out for you is the new covenant in My blood" (Luke 22:20)

  - The Synoptics clearly identify the Last Supper as a Passover meal (Mark 14:12; Luke 22:15)

  - Our conclusion: Jesus ate the Passover early — He could not simultaneously eat the Passover and BE the Passover sacrifice
  - Luke 22:15 supports this: "before I suffer" implies urgency, awareness of timing
  - This is an inference, honestly labeled
  - The alternative reading ("eat the Passover" = broader festival meals) is presented fairly

- The betrayer identified (John 13:21–30) — Judas leaves into the night

## Part Three — The Silence

### Chapter 10: Three Days and Three Nights

**STATUS: NOT STARTED (full source study exists)**

**Source material:** Three Days and Three Nights (10-page study), The Last Week of the Lamb Timeline (Part One)

The forensic heart of the book — the chapter that proves the timeline. This is where the Prologue's brief tease of the math problem gets its full treatment.

- What Jesus said:
  - Matthew 12:40 — "three days and three nights in the heart of the earth" — tied to Jonah 1:17
  - Every other prediction: "on the third day" (Matt 16:21; 17:23; 20:19; Luke 9:22; 18:33; 24:7, 46), "after three days" (Mark 8:31; 9:31; 10:34), "in three days" (John 2:19)
  - His enemies took it literally (Matthew 27:63–64) — "after three days"
- The Friday problem:
  - Friday afternoon to Sunday dawn = 1 full day (Saturday) + parts of Friday and Sunday
  - At most 2 nights (Friday night, Saturday night) — not 3
  - The partial-day argument ("any part of a day counts") — examined and found insufficient: it ignores the "three nights" half of Jesus' own words
  - Jesus did not say "three days." He said "three days AND three nights." He specified both.
- The two-Sabbath evidence:
  - John 19:31 — the Sabbath after the crucifixion was "a high day"
  - Leviticus 23:5–7 — Nisan 15 is a commanded Sabbath regardless of weekday
  - Luke 23:55–56 — women PREPARED spices, THEN rested on the Sabbath
  - Mark 16:1 — women BOUGHT spices AFTER the Sabbath was over
  - You cannot prepare what you haven't purchased — unless there were TWO Sabbaths with a working day between
  - This is the Spice Paradox — and it has only one resolution
- The count:
  - Wednesday burial before sundown
  - Wed sundown → Thu sundown = Night 1, Day 1 (High-Day Sabbath)
  - Thu sundown → Fri sundown = Night 2, Day 2 (Working day — spices bought and prepared)
  - Fri sundown → Sat sundown = Night 3, Day 3 (Weekly Sabbath)
  - After Sat sundown = risen. Tomb found empty Sunday dawn.
  - Three days. Three nights. Exactly as Jesus said.
- The Emmaus Road objection (Luke 24:21) — "the third day since these things happened"
  - "These things" includes the posting of the guard on Thursday (Matt 27:62–66)
  - Counting from Thursday: Fri=1, Sat=2, Sun=3. Fits.
- Mark 16:9 — "early on the first day of the week" — punctuation question, not a word question
  - Greek allows "early on the first day" to modify "appeared," not "risen"
  - Mark 16:9–20 textual variant noted (NASB flags these verses)
  - The text says the tomb was found empty Sunday morning. It does not say He rose Sunday morning.

**Key Scriptures:** Matthew 12:40; 27:62–66; John 19:31; Leviticus 23:5–7; Luke 23:55–56; Mark 16:1; Luke 24:21; Jonah 1:17
