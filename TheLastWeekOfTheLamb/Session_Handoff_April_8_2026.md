### 2. Chapter 1 Written and Verified

Chapter 1 ("The Lamb in Egypt") was written, then corrected through multiple passes for textual accuracy. Final version is in the Prologue voice. Three significant corrections were made:

**The Inspection section:** The original draft said the four-day period (Nisan 10–14) was explicitly for "inspection." Exodus 12:5–6 says "keep it until the fourteenth day." The word is *mishmeret* — to keep, guard, hold in custody. The text does NOT say "inspect for blemishes during this period." The four-day keeping period likely served to confirm the lamb's condition and to create personal attachment (the family knows the animal that dies for them), but the text does not state either purpose explicitly. Chapter 1 now presents both possibilities honestly and says "we are not told" where we are not told.

**The Meal section:** The original draft said unleavened bread was included "because there was no time to let dough rise" and bitter herbs were "a reminder of the bitterness of slavery." Both are traditional explanations, not textual ones. Exodus 12:8 commands unleavened bread and bitter herbs without stating reasons. Exodus 12:39 describes the dough not rising during the departure, but the COMMAND to eat unleavened bread was given BEFORE the departure. The command to remove leaven (Exodus 12:15, 19–20) carries the penalty of being cut off from Israel — this is a serious requirement, not a side effect of haste. Chapter 1 now states what the text says, acknowledges where it is silent on reasons, and lets Paul (the apostle) make the leaven/sin connection via 1 Corinthians 5:7–8 rather than us imposing it on Exodus 12.

**The Leaven section:** New section added giving the leaven removal its proper weight — a command with severe consequences, distinguished from the departure circumstance, with the 1 Corinthians 5:7–8 connection identified as Paul's Spirit-guided application.

### 3. Full Outline Audited for Textual Accuracy

Every chapter entry in the outline was scanned for claims that go beyond the text. Corrections made:

- **"Inspection" language** changed to "kept/keeping" in all Exodus 12 references (10 places). Gospel-side descriptions of Jesus being examined/tested remain, since that IS what the Gospel text records. The connection between the Exodus 12 keeping period and the Gospel examinations is labeled as typological inference.
- **Fig tree symbolism** (Chapter 4) no longer stated as fact. Presented as a parallel the reader can observe.
- **Gethsemane cup** (Chapter 8) now distinguishes textual meaning (cup = suffering/death, per Matthew 20:22–23) from inference (cup = bearing sin of the world, from Isaiah 53/2 Cor 5:21).
- **"Silently" at the arrest** removed — Jesus speaks multiple times during the arrest (Matt 26:52–54; John 18:4–8). Isaiah 53:7 ("He did not open His mouth") applies to the trial phase (Matt 27:12–14; Luke 23:9), not the arrest.
- **Darkness** (Chapter 9) no longer says "creation responding." States the fact; the text doesn't explain the cause.
- **"Forsaken"** (Chapter 9) no longer claims "separation from the Father." Records the words, notes the weight, does not interpret what happened between Father and Son.
- **Tetelestai** (Chapter 9) gives both the textual meaning ("it is finished") and the commercial background ("paid in full") honestly, noting the latter is background context, not the text itself.
- **Veil** (Chapters 9 and 13) now states the text says "from top to bottom" and notes the divine-action inference as widely observed but not explicitly stated.
- **"Exact time"** (Chapter 9) changed to "the hour when the Passover lambs were being slaughtered" — we cannot verify exact timing across 1,500 years of practice.
- **"Not one hour more, not one hour less"** (Chapter 11) removed — we don't know the exact hour of the resurrection.
- **Stone rolled away** (Chapter 12) now shows the textual evidence (tomb already empty, body later passes through walls in John 20:19, 26) and lets the reader draw the conclusion rather than stating it as fact.

## Voice Decision

The **Prologue** is the tuning fork for the entire book. Warm, invitational, walking alongside the reader. "Come and see" rather than "here's why everyone else is wrong." Wonder at what God did, not frustration at what tradition missed. Write as if sitting across from someone at a kitchen table, walking them through something that genuinely amazes you.

The forensic/logical voice has its place (Chapter 10, "Three Days and Three Nights") but it is not the dominant tone.

---

## CRITICAL RULE — Textual Integrity

**This is the most important principle for every chapter going forward.**

Do not state things as fact that the text does not explicitly say. Do not embellish God's story. He has already told it. We must be true to the text.

- Where the text is explicit, say so.
- Where a conclusion requires inference, identify it as inference.
- Where we do not know, say we do not know.
- Where traditional interpretations are commonly held but not stated in the text (bitter herbs = slavery, fig tree = Israel, cup = sin-bearing, veil torn by God, etc.), present them honestly as observations or inferences, not as textual claims.
- Where other Scripture makes a connection (Paul connecting leaven to sin in 1 Cor 5:7–8, Hebrews connecting the veil to Christ's flesh in Heb 10:19–20), let THAT Scripture make the connection and cite it. Scripture interpreting Scripture is our method. Us interpreting Scripture and presenting it as though Scripture said it is not.
- Scholars will try to dissect this work. Every claim must stand on the text. Do not give them ammunition by overclaiming what the text says.

**The standard:** If someone opens a Bible and reads the passage being cited, will they find what we said? If yes, we're good. If no — if what they find is a reasonable inference from the passage but not what it actually says — then we must label it as inference.
