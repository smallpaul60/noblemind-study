## What Was Accomplished This Session

### 1. Chapter 9 Written: The Lamb Is Killed — Nisan 14 (Afternoon)

The crucifixion chapter. Structure:

- Scourging and mockery (Matthew 27:26–30) — text does not describe His response. Gospel writers' restraint became the model.
- Road to Golgotha — Simon of Cyrene (Mark 15:21). Daughters of Jerusalem (Luke 23:27–28).
- Crucifixion — the Gospels do not describe the mechanics. They say "they crucified Him" and move to what happened around the cross.
- Inscription — Pilate refused to change it (John 19:21–22).
- "Father, forgive them" — Greek verb tense indicates repeated, not once (Luke 23:34).
- Soldiers and garments — John identifies Psalm 22:18 fulfillment (John 19:23–24).
- Mockery — "He saved others; He cannot save Himself" noted as the most precise theological statement made that day, without editorializing.
- Two criminals — Luke 23:39–43. One mocked, one believed.
- Darkness noon to 3 PM — all three Synoptics record it. Text does not explain the cause. Noted that it cannot be an eclipse (Passover = full moon).
- "My God, My God, why have You forsaken Me?" — quoting Psalm 22:1. What occurred between Father and Son is not explained. Isaiah 53:6 and 2 Corinthians 5:21 brought alongside. Both left to stand without claiming to see into what the Gospel writers left veiled.
- "It is finished" — *tetelestai*. Both meanings presented: textual ("completed") and commercial background ("paid in full"), honestly distinguished.
- Veil torn — direction stated ("from top to bottom"), divine-action inference labeled as widely noted observation.
- Burial — Joseph of Arimathea, Nicodemus. Courage noted: they came forward after the crucifixion, not before.
- John 19:31 — "high day" planted as seed for Chapter 10. Not unpacked here.
- Spice sequence — NOT quoted in this chapter. Chapter ends at Luke 23:55 (women saw the tomb) and tells reader the spice question will matter in Chapter 10. This was a correction Paul caught — avoids getting ahead of the two-Sabbath argument.
- Exodus 12 closing — day-by-day alignment labeled as typological inference. Gospel events and Exodus commands each labeled as textual fact. 1 Corinthians 5:7 cited as Scripture's own connection.
- AD 31 date NOT mentioned — not yet established in the book. Correction Paul caught.

### 2. Chapter 10 Written: Three Days and Three Nights

The forensic heart of the book. Major structural decision: **Chapter 11 (The Time Between) was absorbed into Chapter 10.** The three days in the tomb are walked through once — both forensically and narratively — inside the count section. No separate chapter retreading the same ground.

Structure:

- What Jesus said — Matthew 12:40 (three days AND three nights, tied to Jonah). All other predictions listed. Enemies took it literally (Matt 27:63–64).
- The Friday problem — two nights, not three. Partial-day argument examined: ignores the "three nights" half.
- Jonah parallel — literal for Jonah, literal for Jesus, or figurative for both. No one has ever suggested Jonah's time was figurative.
- John 19:31 — the high day. Leviticus 23:6–7. Two kinds of Sabbath explained.
- The spice paradox — Luke 23:55–56 and Mark 16:1 placed side by side. "You cannot prepare what you have not yet purchased." Two Sabbaths with a working day between.
- **The count (expanded with narrative weight):**
  - Thursday: high-day Sabbath. Guard posted (Matt 27:62–66). Disciples scattered. Enemies acted. Night 1, Day 1.
  - Friday: working day. Women buy spices (Mark 16:1), prepare them (Luke 23:56a). "They were not preparing for a resurrection. They were preparing for a final goodbye." Night 2, Day 2.
  - Saturday: weekly Sabbath. Women rest (Luke 23:56b). Three days completing. Night 3, Day 3.
  - Sunday dawn: tomb found empty. John 20:1.
- Thursday crucifixion examined and eliminated — back-to-back Sabbaths, no working day for spices, only two nights.
- Emmaus Road objection — "these things" includes posting of the guard on Thursday. Counting from Thursday: Fri=1, Sat=2, Sun=3. Fits.
- Mark 16:9 — punctuation question, not a word question. Textual variant noted.
- Discovery vs. moment — the text describes the discovery, not the moment of resurrection.
- Summary of all evidence — each line of evidence stated with its source.
- **A Word About Tradition:**
  - The day of the crucifixion does not affect anyone's relationship with God.
  - The resurrection on the first day of the week is NOT in question — established with Acts 2:1, 41, 47 (Pentecost), Acts 20:7, 1 Corinthians 16:2, Revelation 1:10.
  - "Palm Sunday," "Ash Wednesday," "Good Friday," and "Easter" do not appear in the Bible. What Jesus gave us to remember His sacrifice was the bread and the cup (Luke 22:19; 1 Corinthians 11:24–25).
  - Tradition not invented in bad faith — arose from natural reading of "day of preparation." Problem is that John told us which Sabbath, and it's been overlooked.
  - Text has authority over tradition.
- **Closing — softened per Paul's correction:** "We believe the evidence points strongly in one direction" — not "the conclusion is simple." Honest, shows our work, lets the reader decide.
- Silence section absorbed from former Chapter 11: the disciples didn't know Sunday was coming. God keeps His word precisely. "The silence was not empty. It was measured."

### 3. Chapter 11 Written: The Stone Moves (formerly Chapter 12)

The resurrection chapter. Structure:

- All four accounts presented, differences acknowledged as natural result of multiple witnesses.
- Angel's words — "just as He said." Fixed per Paul's correction: does NOT say "it was the third day" (by Sunday morning, the third day is over). Says "Three days and three nights passed. He said He would rise. He rose."
- The stone — Emmaus disappearance (Luke 24:30–31), locked doors (John 20:19, 26). Stone moved for the witnesses, not for Him. Labeled as inference.
- Peter and John — linen wrappings lying there, face-cloth folded separately. Not a robbery.
- Mary — "Rabboni!" One word. His name for her. No commentary needed.
- Witnesses — 1 Corinthians 15:3–8. Named individuals. Most of the 500 still alive when Paul wrote.
- Thomas — "My Lord and my God!" Jesus did not correct him.
- Transformation — Peter from denial to Pentecost. "People do not die for what they know to be a lie."
- What the resurrection means — Romans 1:4 (declaration), Romans 4:25 (justification), Acts 2:24 and 1 Corinthians 15:20–22 (death conquered).

### 4. Chapter 12 (The Torn Veil) — ABSORBED INTO EPILOGUE

Originally written as a standalone chapter. Paul identified two problems: (1) it pulled the reader backward chronologically (the veil tore during the crucifixion, already covered in Chapter 9), and (2) it was too narrow — the veil/access was not the only thing accomplished by the death and resurrection; the hope of resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:20–22) was missing. Rather than broadening the chapter and accepting the chronological regression, the strongest elements — the veil (Matthew 27:51; Hebrews 10:19–20), the hope (1 Corinthians 15:20, 22), and the invitation (Acts 2:38, 41) — were absorbed into the Epilogue. Chapter 12 was eliminated. The book is now 11 chapters.

### 5. Epilogue Written: The Thread Completed

Brief. Quiet. Returns to the Prologue thread — Genesis 3:15, Abraham, Judah, David, Isaiah, Moses, the blueprint. Walks through the week one final time in summary. "The seed crushed the serpent's head."

Methodology restated: text speaks for itself, every passage cited, every inference labeled.

"The tradition is old. But the text is older."

Closes with the NobleMind.Study attribution.

## Voice Decision (Unchanged)

The **Prologue** is the tuning fork. Warm, invitational, walking alongside the reader. "Come and see" rather than "here's why everyone else is wrong." Wonder at what God did, not frustration at what tradition missed. Kitchen table register.

## CRITICAL RULES (Unchanged)

**Textual Integrity:** Do not state things as fact that the text does not explicitly say. Where the text is explicit, say so. Where a conclusion requires inference, identify it as inference. Where we do not know, say we do not know.

**Exodus 12 Keeping Period:** The text does NOT say the keeping period was for inspection. The correspondence between the four-day keeping period and the Gospel examinations is our observation, not the text's statement.

**Standalone Work:** This book has no companion studies. All source material is absorbed.
