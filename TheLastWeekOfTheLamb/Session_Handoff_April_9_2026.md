### 2. Chapter 2 Written: The Lamb in Prophecy

Isaiah 53 examined verse by verse. Structure:

- The portrait no one expected — Israel wanted a king, Isaiah described a sacrifice
- 53:1–3: The appearance — despised, forsaken, no stately form
- 53:4–6: The substitution — pierced for OUR transgressions, crushed for OUR iniquities. Every clause has a substitution. "All of us like sheep have gone astray... the LORD has caused the iniquity of us all to fall on Him."
- 53:7: The silence — "like a lamb led to slaughter." The word that connects everything. Reaches back to Exodus 12, forward to the cross.
- 53:8–9: The death — cut off from the living, grave with wicked men, yet with a rich man in death. No violence, no deceit.
- 53:10: The purpose — the LORD was pleased to crush Him. *Asham* (guilt offering). "He will prolong His days" — life after death, stated seven centuries in advance.
- 53:11–12: The outcome — the Righteous One justifies the many. Bore the sin of many. Interceded for transgressors.
- John 1:29 — "Behold, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!" The bridge connecting Exodus 12 (blueprint) + Isaiah 53 (portrait) to a specific person.
- Closing: Three witnesses across centuries — the blueprint, the portrait, the identification — all pointing to the same Lamb.

**Textual integrity verified.** Every claim stays within what Isaiah and John actually say. The *asham* connection is Isaiah's own word. The resurrection inference ("prolong His days") is Isaiah's own statement. No overclaiming.

### 4. Chapter 3 Written: The Arrival and the Selection — Nisan 9–10

The first chapter of Part Two. Covers two days. Structure:

- John's count: "Six days before the Passover" (John 12:1) → inclusive counting explained and demonstrated → arrival in Bethany = Friday, Nisan 9 → Sabbath supper that evening → "on the next day" (John 12:12) = Saturday, Nisan 10 for the entry
- Mark's backward chain: Wednesday anchor → "two days before" = Monday → "in the morning" = Monday → "next day" after temple cleansing = Sunday → "next day" after entry = Saturday. Same result.
- Two paths, one day: Two independent witnesses, different starting points, different methods, same conclusion — Saturday, Nisan 10.
- What happened that day: Mark 11:11 — entered temple, looked around, left. Remarkably restrained. Makes sense on the Sabbath.
- Sabbath entry addressed honestly: Within Sabbath day's journey (Acts 1:12), donkey not labor, Pharisees objected to messianic praise NOT Sabbath violation — significant silence.
- Alternative tested: Exclusive counting → entry on Friday → temple cleansing on Sabbath → no merchants to drive out. Practical absurdity. Inclusive count avoids this.
- Exodus 12 parallel: Nisan 10 = lamb selection day. Clearly labeled as typological inference — "No Gospel writer says 'Jesus entered Jerusalem on Nisan 10.'" Two independent counts landing on the same day as Exodus 12:3 noted as convergence worth pausing over.
- Closing: The Lamb has been selected. Tomorrow the examination begins.

## CRITICAL RULE — Textual Integrity (Unchanged)

Do not state things as fact that the text does not explicitly say. Do not embellish God's story.

- Where the text is explicit, say so.
- Where a conclusion requires inference, identify it as inference.
- Where we do not know, say we do not know.
- Where other Scripture makes a connection, let THAT Scripture make it and cite it.
- The standard: If someone opens a Bible and reads the passage being cited, will they find what we said?
