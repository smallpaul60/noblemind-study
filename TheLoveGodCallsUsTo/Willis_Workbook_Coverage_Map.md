# Willis Workbook × *The Love God Calls Us To*
## Coverage Map — Lesson 16: 1 Corinthians 13

Charles Willis's *1 Corinthians* Back to the Bible workbook covers the
whole letter in twenty-one lessons. *The Love God Calls Us To* (TLGCUT)
is a focused exegesis of 1 Corinthians 13 — sixteen chapters covering
verses 4–13. This map shows how TLGCUT answers the questions of **Lesson
16** (the single workbook lesson devoted to chapter 13), pulling from
both the **Student Workbook** and the **Teacher's Manual**.

---

## Student Workbook — Lesson 16 Questions

### Q1 — What had the Corinthians forgotten in their zeal for spiritual gifts (13:1–3)?

| TLGCUT chapter | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Ch 1 — A More Excellent Way** | Direct exegesis of 13:1–3. Shows why the Corinthians' chase after the showy gifts was a chase after *nothing* without love, and why love is the more excellent way. |

---

### Q2 — For each of the traits describing love, relate how the Corinthians were not showing love in the current behaviors revolving around spiritual gifts.

Every TLGCUT attribute chapter opens with the Greek word, then exegetes
the trait, then has a section explicitly titled or framed as *"What was
happening in Corinth"* before closing with application. So each Willis
sub-question gets both a doctrinal walk-through and the Corinthian
behavior diagnosis.

because Paul uses two distinct verbs.)

### Q3 — 13:9–10 speak of the "part" (or partial) and the "perfect" (or complete). What are these referring to?

| TLGCUT chapter | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Ch 16 — Love Never Fails** | Direct exegesis of 13:9–10. Identifies the *partial* as the apostolic-age means of revelation (prophecy, tongues, knowledge) and the *perfect* (Greek *teleios*) as the completed New Testament canon — with the scaffolding/building metaphor for how the partial gave way to the complete. |

### Q4 — When will miraculous gifts be done away with (13:10)?

| TLGCUT chapter | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Ch 16 — Love Never Fails** *(section: "When will miraculous gifts be done away?")* | Direct answer: when the *perfect* came — when the revelation of God in the New Testament Scriptures was complete. Same chapter, separate sub-section. |

### Q5 — What comparison does Paul use to illustrate his point (13:11)?

| TLGCUT chapter | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Ch 16 — Love Never Fails** | Direct exegesis of 13:11 (the childhood-to-maturity comparison). Frames Paul's metaphor as the church's *infancy* (partial gifts) versus the church's *maturity* (full Word) — not as this-life vs. next-life. |

### Q6 — In context how do "we see in a mirror dimly" (13:12)?

| TLGCUT chapter | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Ch 16 — Love Never Fails** | Direct exegesis of 13:12. Distinguishes two layers: the *completed canon* answers 13:9–10, but the *mirror/face-to-face* in 13:12 reaches further — to the believer's eternal seeing of the Lord. Holds both at once. |

### Q7 — When will we "know fully" (13:12)?

| TLGCUT chapter | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| **Ch 16 — Love Never Fails** | Direct answer in the same exegesis: the full knowing is the believer's *"then"* in glory — at death and consummated at the resurrection. |

**Coverage of Q3–Q7: all five questions answered in Ch 16.** Ch 16 has
explicit subsections for each.

## Teacher's Manual — Lesson 16 Additions

The Teacher's Manual adds three things to the Student Workbook page:
a Class Starter, and two optional Class Activities.

### Class Starter
> *"Ask students: 'Do you love your family?' After they finish laughing,
> ask them, 'How do you demonstrate your love for your family?' Many
> answers will likely be given. Connect these ideas to the discussion on
> love in this chapter, and point out how the 'body of Christ' is a
> family."*

**TLGCUT support.** The body-as-family thread is woven through several
chapters but is most concentrated in **Ch 8 (Love Does Not Seek Its
Own)** and **Ch 12 (Love Bears All Things)**, both of which treat the
local church as the family of God and frame love's outworking in family
terms. A teacher could open by reading a single paragraph from one of
those chapters after the class-starter discussion to anchor the family
analogy in Scripture.

### Class Activity 1 — Demonstrating that miracles have ceased
> *"Present a very brief description of some of the religious groups who
> today believe miraculous gifts continue… Have students demonstrate
> their understanding of chapter 13 by explaining how miracles have
> ceased."*

**TLGCUT support.** **Ch 16** is exactly this argument, laid out in
sequence: the gifts named, the *teleios* identified as the completed
New Testament, the scaffolding/building metaphor, and the childhood-to-
maturity comparison. A student who has read Ch 16 should be able to
articulate the cessation argument from Scripture in a role-play.

### Class Activity 2 — Reviewing the attributes through a marriage/dating frame
> *"Be certain the later half of the chapter has been well covered, and
> understood. If so, review the attributes of love again with the idea
> of a romantic relationship — specifically marriage, perhaps with
> flavors of 'dating.' Help the boys understand what should be their
> proper attitude toward a woman, and the girls to understand what they
> should be looking for in a man. Notice please that 'love' (in the
> chapter) is not about physical attraction!"*

**TLGCUT support.** Every attribute chapter (Ch 2–Ch 15) closes with
application that lands in real relationships — family, marriage,
brotherhood — without sentimentalizing the attribute. A teacher
running this activity could assign one attribute per student and have
each prepare a one-minute "what this looks like in dating/marriage" from
the closing pages of the relevant TLGCUT chapter. The
**class-edition** of TLGCUT, designed for junior-high/high-school use,
makes this application explicit.
