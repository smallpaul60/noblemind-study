## Key Decisions Made This Session

### 1. Chapter 7 — No Day-of-Week Names for Crucifixion

Paul and Pam completed a detailed timeline study ("The Last Week of the Lamb") examining every time marker in the Gospel accounts. The study concludes that a Wednesday crucifixion (Nisan 14) fits all textual constraints, while a Friday crucifixion does not — primarily due to:

- **The spice sequence:** Luke 23:56 says the women prepared spices before resting on the Sabbath. Mark 16:1 says they bought spices after the Sabbath was over. You cannot prepare what you haven't purchased. The only resolution is two Sabbaths with a working day between them.
- **John 19:31:** The Sabbath after the crucifixion was "a high day" — the feast-day Sabbath of Nisan 15, not the weekly Sabbath.
- **Matthew 12:40:** Three days and three nights, tied to Jonah. Friday-to-Sunday does not yield three days and three nights without partial-day arguments.

**Decision for Chapter 7:** Do not argue the Wednesday timeline in this book. Do not name any day of the week for the crucifixion. The original draft said "Friday passed. Saturday passed." This was changed to: "The hours passed. The silence was deafening. It was the longest wait in the history of the world. And then came the first day of the week." A second reference to "Sunday morning" in the body was also changed to "first day of the week." The chapter never names Friday, Saturday, or Sunday — only "the first day of the week," which is what all four Gospels say.

The timeline study will become its own separate Noble Mind publication. The *From the Beginning* book simply avoids contradicting it.

### 2. Chapter 7 — Architecture

The chapter follows this flow:
- Opens in the silence after the burial — despair, hiding, the sealed tomb
- **The Dawn** — Matthew 28:1–6: the earthquake, the angel, the guards struck powerless, "He is not here, for He has risen, just as He said"
- **Mary** — John 20:11–16: Mary weeping at the tomb, mistaking Jesus for the gardener, then He speaks her name — "Mary!" / "Rabboni!" Given significant space as one of the most intimate scenes in Scripture
- **The Witnesses** — Women as first witnesses (counter to fabrication), Thomas and the nail marks (callback to Ch5's "My Lord and my God"), 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 (the 500+ living witnesses), the transformation of skeptics (Peter, Thomas, James, Paul)
- **The Transformation** — Hiding behind locked doors to public proclamation in Jerusalem; no one produced the body; willingness to die for what they saw
- **What the Resurrection Proves** — Four subsections:
  - He Was Who He Said He Was (Romans 1:4 — declared Son of God by resurrection)
  - The Payment Was Accepted (Romans 4:25 — raised for our justification; "the resurrection is the receipt")
  - Death Has Been Conquered (Acts 2:24 — impossible for death to hold Him; 1 Corinthians 15:20–22 — first fruits, "in Christ" seed planted again)
  - The Prophets Were Right (Isaiah 53:10 — "prolong His days" callback from Ch4; Acts 17:31 — God furnished proof by raising Him)
- **The Fact That Demands a Response** — 1 Corinthians 15:17; James 2:19 (demons believe); transition to Acts 2:37 "What shall we do?"

### 3. Chapter 8 — The Response as Unified Pattern

Chapter 8 walks through the New Testament response in order: Hear, Believe, Repent, Confess, Be Baptized. Each is treated as equally necessary. Baptism receives the most space because Scripture gives it the most space.

**Key structural elements:**
- Opens with Acts 2:37 callback from Ch7's closing line
- **Hear** — Romans 10:17; every conversion in Acts begins with hearing
- **Believe** — Mark 16:16; Hebrews 11:6; John 8:24; demons believe (James 2:19 callback from Ch7) — belief that saves is belief that acts
- **Repent** — Acts 2:38; 2 Corinthians 7:10 (godly sorrow vs. worldly sorrow); Acts 17:30 (all people everywhere)
- **Confess** — Romans 10:9–10; Matthew 10:32–33
- **Be Baptized** — The largest section, with four subsections:
  - *What Jesus Commanded* — Matthew 28:19–20; Mark 16:16
  - *What Baptism Is* — Greek *baptizo* = immerse; Romans 6:4 (buried); Acts 8:38–39; Matthew 3:16
  - *What Baptism Does* — **The "in Christ" thread payoff.** Ephesians 1:4 (Ch1), Romans 6:23 (Ch6) → "How do you get into Christ?" → Romans 6:3 and Galatians 3:27 (baptized into Christ) → Ephesians 1:3 (every spiritual blessing in Christ). Also Acts 2:38 (for forgiveness), Acts 22:16 (wash away sins — Saul's conversion given special attention), 1 Peter 3:21 (baptism now saves you)
  - *What the Early Church Did* — Nine conversions, nine baptisms, no exceptions. Urgency emphasized (jailer baptized that hour of the night)
- **Not a Work That Earns — A Faith That Obeys** — Ephesians 2:8–9 affirmed; obedience ≠ earning; Noah (Hebrews 11:7), Jericho (Hebrews 11:30) — God acts at the point of obedience
- **A Life, Not a Moment** — 2 Timothy 4:7; Revelation 2:10; transitions to Chapters 9–10

### 4. Conversion Chart — Removed

Paul requested a chart showing each conversion account with columns for Believed, Repented, Confessed, and Baptized. The chart was built, but when only Believed and Baptized had checkmarks in every row (Repented and Confessed were mostly blank because the text doesn't explicitly record them in most accounts), Paul decided it undermined the point that all five elements are equally necessary. The chart was removed. The prose already carries the nine-for-nine baptism point effectively.

## Voice and Style Notes (unchanged)

- **Tone:** Conversational, direct, warm. Coffee shop, not lecture hall. Gentle — speaking to potential Christians or very new Christians.
- **Scripture:** NASB primary. Block format with full reference. Indented italic blockquotes with bold references. Unpacked for readers who've never seen it.
- **Callbacks:** Explicit references to previous chapters. Creates continuity and reinforcement.
- **Transitions:** Every chapter ends with a natural question pulling into the next.
- **Formatting:** Markdown sections with ## headings. Sub-sections under Baptism in Ch8 used ### headings.
- **No denominational language.** Per PRINCIPLES.md.
- **Honest handling:** Say what the text says. Don't soften, don't hedge, don't add what the text doesn't contain. Willing to say "I don't know."
- **Positive presentation:** Chapter 8 was written to state what Jesus commanded and what the apostles taught — not to disprove other doctrines. Let the text do the heavy lifting.

## Key Principle for Remaining Chapters

No institutional language. Just the picture of the church and the Christian life as the New Testament describes them — warm, honest, and grounded in Scripture.
