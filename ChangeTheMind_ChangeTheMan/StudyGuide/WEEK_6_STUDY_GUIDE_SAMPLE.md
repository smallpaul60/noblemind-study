# Change the Mind, Change the Man — Study Guide

## Week 6: THINK

### Chapter 6 — "Think"

---

## Before the Group Meets

Read Chapter 6 of *Change the Mind, Change the Man*.

Then read the following passages — slowly, more than once — before you come to the group:

- **Romans 12:1-2**
- **Philippians 2:1-8**
- **Philippians 4:8**
- **Colossians 3:1-4**

That's your preparation. Read the chapter. Then sit with those four passages. When you come to the group, come ready to talk about what *they* say — not what you think about them, but what they actually say.

---

## Opening

Have someone read Romans 12:1-2 aloud to the group.

Then ask: **What does Paul say is the mechanism of transformation?**

Don't rush past the answer. It's right there in the text. The transformation Paul calls for is not a transformation of behavior first — it is a transformation of the *mind*. "Be transformed by the renewing of your mind." Everything else follows from that.

This is the hinge of the entire book, and it is the hinge of this study. Every chapter before this one traced how the mind turns away from God. Every chapter after this one traces what happens when it turns back. This week, we sit with the turning itself.

---

## Into the Text

Work through these passages together as a group. The leader reads each passage aloud, then the group works through the questions. Stay in the text. If someone offers an opinion, the leader's job is Freddie Anderson's job: *"That's a good thought — but let's see what the Bible says."*

---

### Passage 1 — Romans 12:1-2

> *"Therefore I urge you, brethren, by the mercies of God, to present your bodies a living and holy sacrifice, acceptable to God, which is your spiritual service of worship. And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what the will of God is, that which is good and acceptable and perfect."*

**Questions:**

1. Paul says "do not be conformed" and "be transformed." What is the difference between being *conformed* to something and being *transformed*? Look at the words themselves — one is being shaped from the outside in; the other is being changed from the inside out. Which one describes what the world does to a person? Which one describes what God does?

2. The word translated "transformed" is the Greek word *metamorphoō* — the same word that gives us "metamorphosis." A caterpillar does not put on a butterfly costume. It becomes a different creature entirely. What does that tell you about the kind of change Paul is describing? Is he talking about modification, or is he talking about something more total than that?

3. Where does the transformation begin, according to this passage? Not in the hands. Not in the habits. It begins in the *mind*. Why does that matter for someone trying to overcome addiction? Why does it matter for someone watching a loved one try?

4. Notice that Paul does not say "be transformed by willpower" or "be transformed by a change of scenery." He says "by the renewing of your mind." What renews a mind? Where does the new way of thinking come from? (Consider the context: Paul has just spent eleven chapters building the case for what God has done. The "therefore" at the beginning of 12:1 is pointing back to everything that came before it.)

---

### Passage 2 — Philippians 2:1-8

> *"Therefore if there is any encouragement in Christ, if there is any consolation of love, if there is any fellowship of the Spirit, if any affection and compassion, make my joy complete by being of the same mind, maintaining the same love, united in spirit, intent on one purpose. Do nothing from selfishness or empty conceit, but with humility of mind regard one another as more important than yourselves; do not merely look out for your own personal interests, but also for the interests of others. Have this attitude in yourselves which was also in Christ Jesus, who, although He existed in the form of God, did not regard equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied Himself, taking the form of a bond-servant, and being made in the likeness of men. Being found in appearance as a man, He humbled Himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross."*

**Questions:**

5. In verse 2, Paul uses four phrases to describe unity: "same mind," "same love," "united in spirit," "intent on one purpose." How many of those four are about how a person *thinks*? What does that tell you about where unity — and disunity — actually begin?

6. Verse 3 says "do nothing from selfishness." The book argues that when the mind drifts from God, the self fills the vacuum, and the substance fills the emptiness the self creates. Read verse 3 again with that in mind. What is the opposite of selfishness according to this verse? And what does that require the mind to be doing?

7. Verse 5 says to have the same *attitude* — the same *mind* — as Christ. Then verses 6-8 describe what that mind looked like in action. Christ did not grasp. He emptied. He served. He submitted. He obeyed — all the way to the cross. What kind of thinking produces that kind of living? And what kind of thinking produces the opposite?

---

### Passage 3 — Colossians 3:1-4

> *"Therefore if you have been raised up with Christ, keep seeking the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God. Set your mind on the things above, not on the things that are on earth. For you have died and your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ, who is our life, is revealed, then you also will be revealed with Him in glory."*

**Questions:**

8. "Set your mind on the things above, not on the things that are on earth." This is a command — not a suggestion, not an aspiration. Paul tells them to *set* their minds. The word implies a deliberate, sustained act of the will. What does it look like, practically, to set your mind on something? And what happens when you don't — when the mind has no fixed point?

9. The book's thesis is that the gaze was the real problem — not the substance. Read verse 2 again with that in mind. If the mind is set on "things above," what is it *not* set on? And if it is not set on anything above, what fills the space?

---

### Passage 4 — Philippians 4:8

> *"Finally, brethren, whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is right, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is of good repute, if there is any excellence and if anything worthy of praise, dwell on these things."*

**Questions:**

10. Freddie Anderson taught that Philippians 4:8 is not where "think" begins — it's where it arrives. Paul the apostle had been building this theme across every letter he wrote, and this verse is the culmination. Look at the list: true, honorable, right, pure, lovely, of good repute, excellent, worthy of praise. These are not random qualities. They describe God and the things of God. What happens to a person's mind — over time — when it is filled with these things? And what was it filled with before?

11. The word translated "dwell on" — or in some translations, "think on" — is the Greek *logizomai*. It means to calculate, to reckon, to take careful account of. It is not casual. It is not daydreaming. It is deliberate, sustained, focused attention. Why does that matter? Why would Paul choose a word that means *calculate* rather than a word that means *consider*?

12. Now read this verse in light of everything you've read today — Romans 12:1-2, Philippians 2:1-8, Colossians 3:1-4. This is not one verse about positive thinking. This is the capstone of an entire theology of the mind. The mind must be renewed (Romans 12). The mind must take on the attitude of Christ (Philippians 2). The mind must be set on things above (Colossians 3). And here, finally: the mind must be filled with what is true, honorable, right, pure, lovely, excellent, and worthy of praise (Philippians 4). What are you filling your mind with? And is it any of these things?

## The Two Tracks

The following questions are for group discussion. Use the track that fits your group, or use both if your group includes both audiences.

---

### Track A — For the One in the Struggle

These questions are between you and the text. Be honest. Nobody in this room is here because they had it all figured out.

A1. The book says: "No program, no facility, no change of environment will hold if your mind doesn't change." You may have tried to quit before — maybe more than once. Looking back, was it your *circumstances* that changed, or was it your *mind*? What is the difference?

A2. Romans 12:2 says the mind is renewed. It does not say the mind renews itself. Where is the renewal coming from in that passage? What does that mean for someone who has tried — and failed — to change by sheer determination?

A3. Colossians 3:2 commands you to set your mind on things above. Be specific: what occupied your mind before? What would it look like — tomorrow morning, not in theory — to begin setting it somewhere else?

A4. Philippians 4:8 gives you a list. True. Honorable. Right. Pure. Lovely. Of good repute. Excellent. Worthy of praise. When your mind is not on these things, what is it on instead? Be specific. Name it honestly, at least to yourself.

---

### Track B — For the Family

These questions speak to your part of the story. You did not choose the addiction, but you are living inside it. And your mind needs renewing too.

B1. Chapter 6 says this is perhaps the hardest truth for the family: you cannot change your loved one's mind for them. You can create conditions, remove obstacles, and pray — but the turning is a choice only they can make. Read Romans 12:1-2 again. Who does the urging? (God, through Paul.) Who does the presenting? (The individual — "present *your* bodies.") What does that tell you about the limits of what you can do for someone else — and about the One who is still at work even when you can't be?

B2. Your mind needs renewing too. Fear, guilt, anger, bargaining, replaying every memory looking for the sign you missed — these are the thought patterns that imprison families. Read Philippians 4:8. The command is not only for the addict. It is for everyone who belongs to Christ. What have you been dwelling on? And what does the text say to dwell on instead?

B3. Philippians 2:3-4 says to regard others as more important than yourselves and to look out for the interests of others. Families sometimes interpret this to mean they should sacrifice everything for the addicted person — including their own wellbeing and the wellbeing of other family members. But read the verse again carefully. Paul is describing *the mind of Christ*, which includes both humility and obedience to the Father's will. How does the "mind of Christ" help you distinguish between self-sacrificing love and destructive enabling? (You may want to look back at what you studied in Chapter 5.)

B4. Colossians 3:2 — "Set your mind on the things above." When the crisis is consuming, "things above" can feel impossibly far away. But the passage does not say "set your mind above when things are easy." It is written to people who had real troubles, real losses, and real reasons to fix their eyes on the ground. What is one thing — from this week's study, from the Scriptures you have read — that you can set your mind on when the fear takes over?

## Memory Verse

> *"And do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what the will of God is, that which is good and acceptable and perfect."*
> — Romans 12:2 (NASB)

Carry this with you this week. Not as a slogan. As a truth.

---

## For the Week Ahead

This is not an assignment. It is a practice.

Each day this week, before anything else occupies your mind in the morning, read Philippians 4:8 once. Then ask yourself — honestly — what your mind was set on yesterday. You don't have to write anything down. You don't have to report to anyone. Just read the verse. Ask the question. Let the text do what the text does.

If you find yourself in a moment of temptation, or a moment of fear, or a moment where the old thinking tries to reassert itself — read it again. Not as a magic formula. As a reminder of where the mind is supposed to be set, and who set it there.

---

## A Note for the Group Leader

Your job this week is not to teach. Your job is to keep the group in the text.

When someone shares an opinion, ask: "Where do you see that in the passage?" When someone drifts into generalities, bring them back: "Let's look at the verse again." When someone opens up honestly about where their mind has been — let them. Don't rush to fix it. The text is already doing the work.

You are doing what Freddie Anderson did. You are asking, "Let's see what the Bible says." And then you are letting it say it.
