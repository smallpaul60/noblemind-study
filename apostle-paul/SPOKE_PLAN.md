# Apostle Paul Timeline — Spoke Plan

Internal planning doc for the chronological deep-dive timelines that hang off the Life timeline (`apostle-paul/index.html`). The Corinth spoke (`corinth.html`) is the model. Excluded from deploy.

## The shape that works

The Corinth spoke succeeds because it has three properties:
1. **Multiple events** — enough to chronologize meaningfully
2. **A multi-year arc** — events span enough time to feel like a "spoke"
3. **Internal thematic unity** — the events tell one coherent story

Spokes that share this shape will work. Spokes that don't will feel sparse or arbitrary.

## Tier 1 — Build first, in this order

### 1. Paul & Ephesus *(strongest sibling to Corinth)*

40-year arc, ~12–15 events, from Paul's first stop to Christ's letter through John.

- **c. AD 52** — Brief stop on the way to Antioch (Acts 18:19–21); promises to return
- **c. AD 53** — Apollos arrives; Priscilla and Aquila instruct him more accurately (Acts 18:24–28)
- **c. AD 53** — Paul returns; meets twelve disciples who knew only John's baptism; baptizes them into the name of the Lord Jesus; they receive the Holy Spirit (Acts 19:1–7)
- **c. AD 53** — Three months reasoning in the synagogue (Acts 19:8)
- **c. AD 53–55** — Two-plus years in the school of Tyrannus; the word of the Lord spreads throughout Asia (Acts 19:9–10)
- **c. AD 55** — Extraordinary miracles; the sons of Sceva incident; magic books burned (Acts 19:11–20)
- **c. AD 55** — Paul writes 1 Corinthians from Ephesus (1 Cor 16:8)
- **c. AD 56** — Paul sends Timothy and Erastus to Macedonia (Acts 19:22)
- **c. AD 56** — The riot of Demetrius and the silversmiths (Acts 19:23–41)
- **c. AD 56** — Paul departs for Macedonia (Acts 20:1)
- **c. AD 57** — Miletus farewell to the Ephesian elders (Acts 20:17–38) — Paul will not see them again
- **c. AD 60–62** — The letter to Ephesus written from Roman house arrest
- **c. AD 63–64** — Paul leaves Timothy at Ephesus to charge the teachers (1 Tim 1:3)
- **c. AD 63–64** — 1 Timothy written to Timothy at Ephesus
- **c. AD 66–67** — Onesiphorus of Ephesus refreshes Paul in his second imprisonment (2 Tim 1:16–18); 2 Timothy references the Ephesian congregation
- **c. AD 95** — Christ's letter to Ephesus through John: "you have left your first love" (Rev 2:1–7)

The progression — fugitive teacher to two-year resident to recipient of Christ's later warning — is theologically the richest spoke in the whole corpus.

### 2. Paul's Roman Years *(the end-of-life arc)*

8-year arc, ~10–12 events. Honest about reconstruction where Acts ends.

- **c. AD 59 (autumn)** — Voyage from Caesarea begins (Acts 27:1)
- **c. AD 59–60 (winter)** — Shipwreck at Malta; three months wintering (Acts 27:13–28:10)
- **c. AD 60 (spring)** — Arrival at Rome via Syracuse, Rhegium, Puteoli; brethren meet Paul at the Forum of Appius and Three Inns (Acts 28:11–16)
- **c. AD 60–62** — Two years under house arrest, preaching the kingdom of God (Acts 28:30–31)
- **c. AD 60–62** — Prison Epistles written: Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, Philemon
- **c. AD 62–63** — Likely release; Acts ends before resolution
- **c. AD 62–66** — Reconstructed fourth missionary period: Crete with Titus (Titus 1:5), Macedonia (1 Tim 1:3), possibly Spain (Rom 15:24, 1 Clement 5)
- **c. AD 63–66** — Pastoral Epistles written: 1 Timothy, Titus, 2 Timothy
- **c. AD 66–67** — Second arrest; second Roman imprisonment, this time without freedom (2 Tim 1:16–17, 4:6–18)
- **c. AD 67** — Martyrdom under Nero (2 Pet 1:13–15 alludes; 1 Clement 5; Eusebius, *Eccl. Hist.* 2.25)

Flag for honesty in the writing: post-Acts events are reconstructed from Pastoral Epistle references and early-church tradition. Mark them as such.

### 3. The Jerusalem Visits *(five visits, each with a different purpose)*

20-year arc, ~10 events. Reveals Paul's evolving relationship with the mother church.

- **c. AD 37** — First visit; Barnabas vouches for him; meets Peter and James the Lord's brother; fifteen days (Gal 1:18–19, Acts 9:26–30)
- **c. AD 46** — Famine relief with Barnabas (Acts 11:27–30; 12:25)
- **c. AD 49** — The Jerusalem Council settles the circumcision question (Acts 15)
- **c. AD 49** — *Sidebar: Peter at Antioch — the rebuke (Gal 2:11–14)*
- **c. AD 52** — Brief visit returning from Second Journey: "went up and greeted the church" (Acts 18:22)
- **c. AD 57** — Pentecost visit; reports to James and the elders; arrested in the temple (Acts 21:17–36)

The progression — fugitive, courier, defendant of the gospel, brief visitor, prisoner — is its own arc.

## Tier 2 — Could come later if Tier 1 works well

### 4. Paul & Antioch (Syrian Antioch)
The sending church. Lighter content than Tier 1 because Antioch's role is mostly *sending* Paul somewhere.
- Barnabas fetches Paul to Antioch (c. AD 43)
- Disciples first called Christians at Antioch
- Famine relief sent (c. AD 46)
- Sent on First Journey (c. AD 47)
- Reports back (c. AD 48)
- Peter incident (c. AD 49)
- Sent on Second Journey (c. AD 49)
- ~7 events.

### 5. Paul's Conversion in Three Tellings
**Non-chronological spoke** — different shape from Corinth. A single-event deep-dive comparing Acts 9 (Luke's narrative), Acts 22 (Paul's defense to the Jerusalem mob), and Acts 26 (Paul's defense to Agrippa). Each retelling emphasizes different facts for different audiences. Would need a different layout — possibly a parallel-columns table rather than a timeline spine.

## What I'd skip (for now) and why

- **Paul & Peter as a spoke** — only three named interactions (Acts 9:26, Acts 15, Gal 2:11–14). Too sparse for its own timeline. Fold into the Jerusalem Visits spoke as sidebars.
- **A Galatia spoke** — the events are spread across First, Second, Third Journeys plus the Galatians letter, but the spoke would mostly duplicate Life-timeline content without much new internal density.
- **The Prison Epistles as their own spoke** — the four letters were all written from one place (Rome) over c. 2 years. That's a cluster, not an arc. Better integrated into the Roman Years spoke.
- **A Pauline Chronology Anchors spoke** (Aretas, Gallio, Agrippa, Tiberius) — would be useful but more academic-apparatus than narrative. Could be an *appendix* on the Life timeline instead of a spoke.

## Implementation notes

- File names follow the pattern `apostle-paul/<slug>.html` (lowercase, no `paul-` prefix since the directory implies it).
- Each spoke is built on the corinth.html template — same parchment aesthetic, same filter UI, same event-card layout, same back-link convention.
- Each spoke needs a corresponding event reference *back* into the Life timeline via `DEEPDIVE_*` constants — when an event in the Life timeline can be deep-dived, add a `deepDive: DEEPDIVE_<NAME>` field as we did for Corinth.
- PDFs can be added later, following the same pattern as `Paul_Life_Timeline.pdf` and `Paul_Corinth_Timeline.pdf`.

## Order of work

1. **Ephesus** first — strongest content, most natural sibling to Corinth, validates that the shape generalizes.
2. **Roman Years** second — different in tone (more reconstruction, more honest hedging needed) but rich material.
3. **Jerusalem Visits** third — the integrative spoke that ties together multiple journeys.
4. Tier 2 only if Tier 1 lands well with readers.
