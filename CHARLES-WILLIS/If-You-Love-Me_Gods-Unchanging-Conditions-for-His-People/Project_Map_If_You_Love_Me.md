# Project Map: *If You Love Me*

**Full title:** *If You Love Me: God's Unchanging Conditions for His People*

**Subtitle / positioning:** A companion to *God's Conditions* by Charles Willis

**Author:** Paul Hainline

**Status as of this writing:** Preface drafted. Chapters 1–2 drafted. Chapters 3–10 and appendices remaining.

---

## Project Concept

This book is the prose companion to Charles Willis's workbook *God's Conditions* (2026). The workbook was written first; this book exists to do what a workbook cannot — engage the systems and traditions that have grown up around the texts the workbook treats, expand each lesson into sustained argument, handle the major proof-texts of the doctrines the book contests, and bring the whole to a landing in a closing chapter that addresses the reader's own response.

The two volumes are designed to be used together. Either order works. The book's structure parallels the workbook's nine-lesson structure, with the addition of a Preface, a closing capstone chapter, and supporting appendices.

---

## The Discipline (binding for every chapter)

Every chapter of this book observes the following commitments, drawn from the *Bible Study Principles* document in the project files and applied at drafting time:

1. **Scripture is the sole authority.** When Scripture conflicts with denominational confession, scholarly consensus, popular tradition, or commentary, Scripture wins.
2. **Post-apostolic vocabulary is set aside.** Phrases like "sinner's prayer," "once saved, always saved," "rapture," "sprinkling" (as baptism), "asking Jesus into your heart," and "eternal security" are engaged as system-products where the book engages them at all, never assumed as Scripture's language.
3. **Sources are verified, quotations are accurate.** Historical citations (Calvin, Westminster, Dort, Augustine, etc.) are checked against the actual texts before being put in quotation marks. Translation issues are named in footnotes where they exist.
4. **NASB is the working translation.** Other translations are consulted for comparison; Greek and Hebrew are referenced when relevant.
5. **The text is allowed to be hard.** Where a passage genuinely is difficult, or where faithful students have disagreed, the book says so honestly rather than forcing a conclusion the text does not yield.

### Main Body — Chapters Paralleling the Workbook

**Chapter 1 — The Teachings of Man About Election**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 1*
- Names the system (TULIP / unconditional election) and traces its lineage from Augustine through Calvin, Dort, Westminster, and into modern Reformed teaching.
- Walks through every NT use of *proorizō* (six occurrences) and shows the system has built a doctrine of *who* on a word Scripture uses for *what*.
- Treats Ephesians 1:3–14 and Romans 8:28–30 as the principal proof-texts.
- Argues from the impartiality of God against unconditional election; addresses the total-depravity counter-argument from Romans 10:13–17.
- Closes by addressing what unconditional election does to the conscience.
- *Status: drafted, with footnoted historical references*

**Chapter 2 — Conditional Statements About Obedience (Old Testament)**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 2*
- Major passages: Genesis 2:15–17, Deuteronomy 6, 7:9–11, 8:19–20, 30:17–20; Leviticus 26; Jeremiah 7:5–7, 29:13.
- Establishes that conditions are not occasional in the Old Testament — they are the texture of how God related to His people from the beginning.
- Sets up the through-line argument: God's character of stating conditions is unchanged in the New Covenant.
- Will need a brief transitional bridge from Chapter 1 (the God who has just been defended against the Calvinist system is the same God who states these conditions).
- *Status: drafted, with engagement of Reformed federal theology (covenant of works / covenant of grace) and a Cocceius footnote*

**Chapter 3 — Conditional Statements About Obedience (New Testament)**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 3*
- Major passages: Matthew 7:21, 16:24–26, 19:17; Luke 6:46; John 15:4, 15:10; Romans 2:6–10; Hebrews 10:36; 1 John 1:6–7.
- The hinge of the book's argument: the conditional pattern of the Old Testament continues unbroken into the New, and Galatians 3:25 (the Law as tutor) plus Malachi 3:6 (God does not change) are the keys.
- Engages "faith only" head-on, particularly James 2:24 — the only verse in the Bible containing the phrase "faith alone" — and shows it is preceded by "not."
- Treats the standard "believe and be saved" texts (Acts 16:31, Romans 10:9, John 3:16) in their actual contexts.

**Chapter 4 — Conditional Warnings**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 4*
- Walks through warnings that name a condition with a stated consequence: Matthew 6:14–15, John 8:24, Luke 13:3, Hebrews 6:4–6, 10:26–31, etc.
- Develops the structure that "stated condition" + "stated promise" implies "unstated warning" — Charles's framework from the workbook, given full prose treatment.
- The chapter where Hebrews 6 and Hebrews 10 do their heaviest work, since those passages cannot be accommodated by the systems the book contests.

**Chapter 5 — Conditions of the Heart and Living**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 5*
- Major passages: Matthew 5:8, 6:21, 12:34–35, 22:37; 1 John 1:9; James 4:8.
- The chapter that addresses the inner life — that the conditions are not external rule-keeping but the orientation of the heart, and that obedience is the natural response of a heart given to God.
- A chapter for the inner life and the conscience, balancing the more polemical Chapters 1, 3, and 4.

**Chapter 6 — Conditions About Salvation**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 6*
- The chapter where the book lays out, plainly, what Scripture says about how a person is saved: hear, believe, repent, confess, be baptized, live faithfully, in Christ.
- Engages the "sinner's prayer," "ask Jesus into your heart," and "faith only" doctrines as direct competitors to the New Testament pattern.
- Treats baptism passages (Acts 2:38, 22:16; Romans 6:3–4; 1 Peter 3:21; Galatians 3:27; Colossians 2:12) at length.
- Probably the longest chapter; takes the most disputed ground.

**Chapter 7 — Conditions About Prayer**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 7*
- Major passages: Matthew 6:5–15; John 9:31, 15:7; James 1:6–7, 4:3, 5:16; 1 John 3:22, 5:14–15; 1 Peter 3:7.
- A chapter where many readers will find unfamiliar territory. Most have never been taught that prayer has conditions; many have been taught that "all you have to do is ask." The texts say otherwise.

**Chapter 8 — Conditions About Falling Away**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 8*
- The direct chapter against once-saved-always-saved. Hebrews 3:12–14; 6:4–6; 10:26–31; 2 Peter 2:20–22; Galatians 5:4; 1 Corinthians 9:27; Revelation 2:5; 22:19; John 15:1–6.
- Addresses every common counter-text used to defend eternal security: John 10:28–29, Romans 8:38–39, etc.
- Pairs naturally with Chapter 1 (election) — the book's two most polemical chapters bracket the more constructive middle chapters.

**Chapter 9 — Conditional Requirements**
*Parallels Workbook Lesson 9*
- Specific commands and qualifications: elders (1 Timothy 3, Titus 1), deacons (1 Timothy 3:8–13), the Lord's Supper (1 Corinthians 11), the role of women in the assembly, marriage and divorce.
- The chapter most likely to overlap with congregational practice questions. Treats each requirement as God's own stated condition.
- Closes the parallel-to-workbook section.

### Capstone

**Chapter 10 — If You Love Me**
*New material; closes the book*
- The reader's own position in light of everything the book has presented.
- Returns to John 14:15 (the title verse) and treats it as the question every honest reader must answer.
- Brief but serious. Not a sermon; a closing word that respects the reader's intelligence and asks for the only response the book has been pointing toward.
- Where appropriate, invites the reader who realizes he has not been baptized into Christ, has not repented, has fallen away, or has been resting in unconditional election to consider what Scripture actually invites him to do.

### Back Matter

**Appendix A — The Principal Proof-Texts of Once-Saved-Always-Saved and Faith-Only**
- A reference appendix collecting the verses most often cited in defense of these doctrines, each treated briefly in context.
- John 10:28–29; Romans 8:28–39; Ephesians 2:8–9; Romans 10:9; Acts 16:31; John 3:16; 1 John 5:13.
- Allows the reader to find concentrated treatment of each verse without having to hunt through chapters.

**Appendix B — A Note on Vocabulary**
- Short list of post-apostolic phrases the book avoids and why.
- Drawn directly from the *Bible Study Principles* document.
- Serves as a tool for the reader who wants to apply the same discipline in his own thinking.

## Estimated Length

- Preface: ~5 pages
- Chapters 1–9: average ~25 pages each (range 20–35; Chapters 6 and 8 likely longer)
- Chapter 10: ~10–15 pages
- Appendices: ~15–20 pages combined
- **Estimated total: 250–300 pages**

This is a normal length for a serious theological book — substantial enough to do real work, not so long that it overwhelms the reader who is also working through the workbook.

## Drafting Process

For each chapter going forward:

1. **Search project knowledge** for the corresponding workbook lesson and any completed lesson document.
2. **Verify any historical or external references** by web search rather than relying on memory. This applies especially to creeds, confessions, historical figures, dates, and quoted material.
3. **Draft the chapter in markdown** following the same structure as Chapter 1 — sustained prose, section subheadings, block quotes for long source material, footnotes for verifiable references.
4. **Flag 2–3 places** at the end of the draft where Paul's eye is particularly wanted — interpretive choices, places the argument stretches, or transitions worth checking.
5. **Wait for review.** Revise based on feedback. Approve before moving to the next chapter.

After all chapters and appendices are drafted and approved, build the full book as a single PDF for publication, with consistent typography, table of contents, and chapter ordering.

## Notes on Voice and Posture

- **The author is Paul.** Where personal voice appears (Preface, Capstone), it is Paul's voice. Claude drafts; Paul owns.
- **The reader is treated as a serious student.** Not condescended to, not lectured, not handled with kid gloves. The reader is assumed capable of reading texts and drawing conclusions.
- **The tone is direct without being combative.** The book argues hard, but it argues from texts, and it does not call names. Disagreement is named clearly; persons are not attacked.
- **Care for the reader is real.** The book's purpose is not to win arguments but to put readers in front of God's word so they can be saved by responding to it. Where chapters land with care for the reader (the closing of Chapter 1, much of Chapters 5 and 10), that care is the genuine motive of the whole project.
